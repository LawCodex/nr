## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title and commencement

This Act may be cited as the Family Court Act 1973 and came into effect on 3 October 1977.

## PART 2 — THE FAMILY COURT

#### 3 The Family Court established

(1) There is hereby established a subordinate court to be called the Family Court.

(2) The Family Court shall be a court of record.

#### 4 Members and Deputy Registrar of the Family Court

(1) The members of the Family Court shall be the person for the time being holding, or acting in, the office of Resident Magistrate or a person appointed under subsection (2A) and such other persons as may be appointed by the President, after consultation with the Chief Justice, to be members.

[subs (1) am Act 9 of 2005 s 4, opn 21 Oct 2005]

(2) The person for the time being holding, or acting in, the office of Resident Magistrate shall be the Chairperson of the Family Court.

(2A) Where the Resident Magistrate is unavailable for whatever reason, a person may be appointed by the President after consultation with the Chief Justice to act as Chairperson of the Family Court during the period of unavailability of the Resident Magistrate.

[subs (2A) insrt Act 9 of 2005 s 4, opn 21 Oct 2005]

(3) Each member, other than the Resident Magistrate, shall be paid the fees and allowances determined by the Minister by Gazette notice.

[subs (3) subst Act 19 of 2011 s 3 and Sch[3], opn 3 Nov 2011]

(4) Save as may be otherwise expressly provided, the Family Court shall be properly constituted for the hearing and determination of any proceedings before it when it is presided over by the Chairperson and 2 or more other members, and not otherwise.

(5) The Deputy Registrar of the District Court shall be the Deputy Registrar of the Family Court.

[subs (5) am Act 17 of 2020 s 5, opn 15 Nov 2020]

[s 4 am Act 17 of 2020 s 5, opn 15 Nov 2020]

#### 5 Decisions of the Family Court

(1) The decision of the majority of the members of the Family Court hearing any cause or matter on any question of fact shall be the decision of the court:

Provided that, where the members are equally divided in their views on the decision which should be given, the Chairperson’s decision shall be the decision of the court.

(2) The decision of the Chairperson on any question of law shall be the decision of the Family Court.

(3) For the purpose of this Section, the decision as to the order to be made in any cause or matter shall be deemed to be a decision on a question of fact.

#### 6 Powers of the Family Court

The Family Court and the members constituting that court shall exercise the jurisdiction, powers and authorities conferred on it by this Act, by the Maintenance Act 1959, by the Adoption of Children Act 1965 and by any other written law.

[s 6 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[67], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 7 General powers of members of the Family Court

Every member of the Family Court shall have power:

(a) to administer oaths and take solemn affirmations and declarations;

(b) to make such orders;

(c) to issue such process; and

(d) exercise such powers, judicial and administrative, in relation to the administration of justice as shall from time to time be prescribed by any written law.

#### 8 Power to compel the attendance of witnesses

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Family Court in criminal proceedings shall have the same powers to compel the attendance of witnesses as are vested in the District Court in criminal proceedings under the Criminal Procedure Act 1972.

(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act the Family Court, in any proceedings other than criminal proceedings, shall, unless other provision is made by any other written law, have the same powers to compel the attendance of witnesses as are vested in the District Court in civil proceedings under the District Court Act 2018.

#### 9 Powers to deal with contempt of the Family court

(1) Whare any person:

(a) wilfully insults a member of the Family Court or any witness or any officer of the court in court, or in going to or returning from court;

(b) wilfully interrupts the proceedings of the Family Court or otherwise misbehaves in court; or

(c) wilfully and without lawful excuse disobeys any order or direction of the Family Court in the course of the hearing of any proceedings, the Deputy Registrar, with or without the assistance of any police officer or other person, may, by order of the Chairperson, take the offender into custody and detain him or her until the rising of the court and the Chairperson may, if he or she thinks fit, convict the offender of contempt of court and commit him or her to prison for any period not exceeding 10 days or impose upon him or her a fine not exceeding $50 for each offence.

[subs (1) am Act 17 of 2020 s 5, opn 15 Nov 2020]

(2) A person aggrieved by an order of the Chairperson under this Section may appeal against it to the Supreme Court within 14 days of the order being made and the provisions of the Bail Act 2018 relating to bail pending the hearing of an appeal from the District Court following conviction shall apply mutatis mutandis. s 6LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

(3) The Supreme Court shall have the same powers to deal with contempt of the authority of the Family Court as it has to deal with contempt of its own authority.

#### 10 Procedure

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, where the Family Court hears and determines a criminal charge, it shall follow as nearly as possible the procedure prescribed in Part VI of the Criminal Procedure Act 1972 for the trial of offences by the District Court.

(2) [subs (2) omitted by the Law Revision Commission under powers authorised by Act 10 of 2019]

(3) Where the Family Court hears and determines or otherwise deals with, any cause or matter in exercise of powers or authorities conferred upon it by any other written law, it shall, unless the procedure which it is to follow is prescribed by that written law, follow as nearly as possible and so far as it may be applicable the procedure prescribed for the District Court in the exercise of its civil jurisdiction.

(4) Notwithstanding the provisions of the other subsections of this Section, the Chief Justice may by rules of court prescribe the procedure to be followed by the Family Court in any type of proceedings or in any part of any proceedings and, where any such rules of court have been made, the provisions of the other subsections of this Section shall, for so long as those rules are in force, have force, and effect only to the extent that they are not repugnant to, or inconsistent with, those rules.

#### 11 Absence of member

(1) Where the hearing of any cause or matter has commenced before the Family Court constituted by 3 or more members and before it has been determined one of the members, other than the Chairperson, dies or, because of illness or absence from the Republic, is unable to continue to attend and is unlikely to be able to do so within a reasonable time, the remaining members may complete the hearing and determine the cause or matter.

(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of Section 4, where any hearing has been completed and the cause or matter determined under the provisions of this Section, the Family Court shall be deemed to have been properly constituted at all times during the hearing and determination of that cause or matter.

#### 12 Language of the Family Court

The language of the Family Court shall be English, unless in any proceedings the Chairperson directs that it shall be Nauruan.

#### 13 Copies of and access to records of evidence

(1) A party to any cause or matter in the Family Court shall be entitled, upon payment of the prescribed fee, to receive a copy of the record of the evidence given therein. s 13 FAMILY COURT ACT 1973

(2) Save as may be provided by any written law, no person shall be entitled as of right to inspect the original record of the proceedings in any cause or matter in the Family Court but such inspection may be authorised by the Chairperson.

#### 14 Recording of evidence

(1) In every cause and matter the whole of any oral evidence given before the Family Court, or so much thereof as the court deems material, shall be taken down in writing by the Chairperson or recorded in such other manner as the Chief Justice may from time to time by rules of court direct.

(2) Oral evidence shall ordinarily be taken down in the form of a narrative and not in the form of questions and answers:

Provided that the Chairperson may in his or her discretion take down, or cause to be taken down, any particular question and answer.

#### 15 Times of sittings of the Family Court

(1) So far as practicable no sitting of the Family Court shall be held at a time when, by reason of another court sitting on that day, persons attending the Family Court will be brought into contact with persons attending any other court.

(2) Subject to the provisions of subsection (1) and to any directions given by the Chief Justice, the Family Court shall sit at such times as the Chairperson directs.

(3) The Family Court shall sit at the Court House at Yaren, unless the Chairperson for any special reason considers that for the hearing or determination of any proceedings it should sit elsewhere.

#### 16 The Family Court not to sit on certain days

The Family Court shall not sit on Christmas Day, Good Friday, Independence Day, Constitution Day, Angam Day or any Sunday.

#### 17 Vacations

The Chief Justice may, by order, direct such vacations of the Family Court, not exceeding in the aggregate 30 days in any one year, as he or she thinks fit.

#### 18 Proceedings of the Family Court not open to the public

(1) No person shall be entitled or permitted to be present at the hearing of any proceedings of the Family Court, save as a witness therein, except:

(a) members and officers of that court;

(b) the persons immediately concerned with the proceedings and their legal representatives;

(c) the parents or guardians of any children in respect of whom the proceedings are taken, or any other person whom the court may admit as standing in the place of such parents or guardians;

(d) any probation officer or welfare officer, whose presence is requested by the court or by any person immediately concerned in the proceedings; and

(e) such other persons as the court may specially authorise to be present. s 13LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

(2) Save with the special leave of the Chairperson, it shall not be lawful for any person to publish a report of any proceedings of the Family Court or the names of the parties thereto or any information by which they may be identifiable.

#### 19 Summoning witnesses

(1) Subject to any other written law, in any cause or matter in the Family Court the Chairperson may, either of his or her own motion or on the application of any party, summon any person within the Republic:

(a) to attend to give evidence; or

(b) to produce any document in his or her possession or power;

(c) may examine such person as a witness; and

(d) require the witness to produce any document in his or her possession or power, subject to just exceptions.

(2) The provisions of Part 8 of the District Court Act 2018 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the Family Court.

#### 20 Seal of the Family Court

The Family Court shall have and use, as occasion may require, a seal of such nature and pattern as the President may, by notice in the Gazette, direct.

#### 21 Members of the Family Court to take oath

Every member of the Family Court shall, before entering upon his or her office, take and subscribe before the Minister the oath or affirmation set out in the Schedule:

Provided that failure by any member to take the oath or affirmation shall not invalidate any proceedings heard and determined by the Family Court.

#### 22 Records and returns

(1) The Family Court shall keep written records and furnish returns of causes and matters heard or dealt with by it to the Supreme Court in such manner as the Chief Justice may from time to time direct.

(2) A Judge of the Supreme Court shall periodically inspect the records of the Family Court and may give such instructions and advice thereon as he or she may deem necessary.

#### 23 Representation of parties

A party to any cause or matter in the Family Court may employ as his or her legal representative, a legal practitioner.

#### 24 Rules relating to dress

The Chief Justice may, by order, prescribe the dress to be worn by members of the Family Court and by a legal practitioner appearing before the Court.

#### 25 Duties of the Deputy Registrar

The duties of the Deputy Registrar of the Family Court shall be:

(a) to attend at such sittings of the court as the Chairperson may direct;

(b) to prepare summonses, warrants, decrees, orders, recognizances, writs of s 25 FAMILY COURT ACT 1973 execution and other documents and to submit them to the Chairperson or, if the Chairperson so directs, to another member of the court, for signature;

(c) to issue process if authorised by rules of court;

(d) to maintain case files and registers, to record judgments and orders of the court and to make, or cause to be made, copies of proceedings when required to do so;

(e) to receive all fees, fines and penalties, and all other moneys paid or deposited in respect of proceedings in the court, and to keep an account of them; and

(f) to perform or cause to be performed, such other duties connected with the court as may be assigned to him or her by the Chairperson.

## PART 3 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 26 Commissioner of Police to be responsible for service and

execution of process of the Family Court

(1) The Commissioner of Police shall be responsible for the service and execution of all such summonses, writs, warrants, orders, commands and process of the Family Court as he or she shall be required by the court or any member thereof to serve or execute and shall make return of every writ, warrant, order, command or process together with the manner of execution thereof to the court:

Provided that, where the court directs or awards any process against the Commissioner of Police or awards any process in any cause, matter or thing in which the Commissioner of Police has such personal interest that the court considers it undesirable that he or she should be responsible for its execution, the Minister may appoint some other fit person to execute and return it and the said process shall be directed to the person so appointed.

(2) Where the Commissioner of Police is prevented, by reason of his or her absence from the Republic or by illness, from performing his or her duties under subsection (1), they shall be performed by the next most senior officer of the Nauru Police Force present in the Republic.

(3) Every writ, warrant, order, command and process may be executed according to its tenor.

#### 27 Police to obey orders and directions of members of the Family

Court All officers of the Nauru Police Force shall obey the orders and obey the orders and directions of every member of the Family Court in the exercise of the criminal jurisdiction of that court and shall have the powers and authority necessary to enable them to do so.

#### 28 Moneys, etc, deposited in the Family Court

(1) All moneys paid into the Family Court to stand to the credit of any party in any cause or matter in the court shall be received into, and credited to, the Courts Trust Fund and paid by the Deputy Registrar into the bank account of that fund.

[subs (1) am Act 17 of 2020 s 5, opn 15 Nov 2020]

(2) All securities for money deposited in the Family Court shall be handed by the Deputy Registrar to the Registrar of Courts who shall place them in safe custody.

[subs (2) am Act 17 of 2020 s 5, opn 15 Nov 2020]

(3) The provisions of Sections 96, 97, 98 and 99 of the Supreme Court Act 2018 shall apply to moneys paid into the Family Court to stand to the credit of parties to causes and matters in that court and to securities deposited in the Family Court.

#### 29 Rules of court

The Chief Justice may make rules of court for the Family Court for all or any of the purposes for which he or she is empowered to make rules of court under the Criminal Procedure Act 1972, District Court Act 2018, Supreme Court Act 2018, Nauru Court of Appeal 2018 and any other written law.
