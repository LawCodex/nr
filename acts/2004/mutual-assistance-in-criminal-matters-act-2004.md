## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title and commencement

(1) This Act may be cited as the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act 2004.

(2) This Act comes into force on certification by the Speaker and came into effect on 3 November 2004.

#### 2 Objectives

The objectives of this Act are:

(a) to regulate the provision by the Republic of international assistance in criminal matters when a request is made by a foreign country for any of the following:

(i) the taking of evidence or the production of a document or other article for a proceeding in the foreign country;

(ii) the issue of a search warrant and seizure of any thing relevant to a proceeding or investigation in the foreign country;

(iii) the forfeiture or confiscation of property for the commission of a serious offence against the law of the foreign country; and

(iv) the restraining of dealings in property that may be forfeited or confiscated because of the commission of a serious offence against the law of the foreign country;

(b) to facilitate the Republic providing international assistance in criminal matters when a request is made by a foreign country to make arrangements for a person who is in the Republic to travel to the foreign country:

(i) to give evidence in a proceeding; or

(ii) to give assistance for an investigation; and

(c) to facilitate the Republic obtaining similar international assistance in criminal matters.

#### 4 Act does not limit other provision of assistance

This Act does not prevent the provision or obtaining of international assistance in criminal matters other than assistance of a kind that may be provided or obtained under this Act.

## PART 2 — REQUESTS FOR ASSISTANCE GENERALLY

#### 6 Requests by the Republic for assistance generally

A request for international assistance in a criminal matter that the Republic is authorised to make under this Act may be made only by the Minister.

#### 7 Requests by foreign countries for assistance generally

(1) Under this Act, a request by a foreign country for international assistance in a criminal matter may be made to the Minister.

(2) A request shall be in writing or by electronic mail and shall include, or be accompanied by, the following information:

(a) the name of the authority concerned with the criminal matter to which the request relates;

(b) a description of the nature of the criminal matter and a statement setting out a summary of the relevant facts and laws;

(c) a description of the purpose of the request and of the nature of the assistance being sought; and

(d) any information that may assist in giving effect to the request.

(3) Failure to comply with subsection (2) is not a ground for refusing the request, but the Minister is not obliged to consider the request until that subsection is complied with.

(4) Where a foreign country makes a request to the court for international assistance in a criminal matter:

(a) the court shall refer the request to the Minister; and

(b) the request is then taken, for this Act, to have been made to the Minister.

#### 8 Assistance may be provided in whole or in part and subject to

conditions Assistance under this Act may be provided to a foreign country in whole or in part and subject to any conditions that the Minister determines.

#### 9 Refusal or postponement of assistance

The Minister may, in respect of any request from a foreign country for mutual assistance in any investigation commenced or proceeding instituted in that foreign country relating to a criminal matter:

(a) refuse the request, in whole or in part, on the ground that to grant the request would be likely to prejudice the sovereignty, security or other essential public interest of the Republic; or

(b) after consulting with the relevant authority of the foreign country, postpone the request, in whole or in part, on the ground that granting the request immediately would be likely to prejudice the conduct of an investigation or proceeding in the Republic.

## PART 3 — ASSISTANCE WITH TAKING EVIDENCE AND PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS OR OTHER ARTICLES

#### 10 Requests by the Republic for assistance with evidence

(1) The Minister may request the appropriate authority of a foreign country to arrange, for a proceeding or investigation in a criminal matter in the Republic, for:

(a) evidence to be taken in the foreign country under the law of that country; or

(b) a document or other article in the foreign country to be produced under the law of that country.

(2) When making a request under subsection (1), the Minister may also request that an opportunity be given for the person giving the evidence or producing the document or other article to be examined or cross-examined, through a video or internet link, from the Republic by:

(a) a party to the proceeding, or the party’s legal representative; or

(b) a person being investigated, or the person’s legal representative.

#### 11 Requests by foreign countries for assistance with evidence

(1) Where a foreign country (the ‘requesting country’) asks that evidence be taken in the Republic for a proceeding or investigation in a criminal matter in the requesting country or another foreign country, the Minister may authorise:

(a) the taking of the evidence; and

(b) the transmission of the evidence to the requesting country.

(2) Where the requesting country asks that a document or other article in the Republic be produced for a proceeding or investigation in a criminal matter in the requesting country or another foreign country, the Minister may authorise:

(a) the production of the documents or other articles; and

(b) their transmission to the requesting country.

#### 12 Taking evidence

(1) Where the Minister authorises the taking of evidence under Section 11(1), a Judge may take, on oath, the evidence of each witness in the matter, and the Judge who takes that evidence shall:

(a) cause the evidence to be put in writing and certify that he or she took the evidence; and

(b) send the evidence and certificate to the Minister.

(2) The evidence of a witness may be taken in the presence or absence of the person to whom the proceeding in the requesting country relates or in the presence or absence of his or her legal representative.

(3) A certificate issued by a Judge under subsection (1)(a) shall state whether, when the evidence was taken, any of the following persons were present:

(a) the person to whom the proceeding in the requesting country relates or his or her legal representative; or

(b) any other person giving evidence or his or her legal representative.

#### 13 Production

(1) Where the Minister authorises the production of a document under Section 11(2), a Judge:

(a) may require the document to be produced to him or her; and

(b) if it is produced, shall send it, or a copy of it certified by the Judge or the Registrar of Courts to be a true copy, to the Minister.

(2) Where the Minister authorises the production of an article other than a document under Section 11(2), a Judge:

(a) may require it to be produced to him or her; and

(b) if the article is produced, shall send it to the Minister.

#### 14 Conduct of proceedings

(1) The Judge conducting a proceeding under Section 11, 12 or 13 may permit any of the following to have legal representation at the proceeding:

(a) the person to whom the proceeding in the requesting country relates;

(b) any other person giving evidence or producing a document or other article at the proceeding before the Judge; or

(c) the relevant authority of the requesting country.

(2) Where the requesting country has so requested, the Judge may permit examination or cross-examination, through a video or internet link from the requesting country, of any person giving evidence or producing a document or other article at the proceeding by:

(a) any person to whom the proceeding in the requesting country relates or by that person’s legal representative; or

(b) the legal representative of the relevant authority of the requesting country.

#### 15 Exceptions to compulsion

(1) The laws of the Republic about:

(a) compelling a person to attend before a Judge; and

(b) giving evidence, answering questions and producing documents or other articles, on the hearing of a charge against a person for an offence against the law of the Republic, apply, so far as they are capable of applying, to the compelling of a person under this Part:

(i) to attend before a Judge; and

(ii) to give evidence, answer questions and produce documents or other articles.

(2) For the purposes of this Part:

(a) the person to whom the proceeding in the requesting country relates is competent, but not compellable, to give evidence; and

(b) the person is not compellable to answer a question or to produce a document or article if the person would not be compellable to do so in the requesting country or other foreign country to which the request relates. s 13LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 4 — ASSISTANCE FOR SEARCH AND SEIZURE

#### 17 Requests by the Republic for search and seizure

(1) This Section applies to a proceeding or investigation for a criminal matter involving a serious offence against the laws of the Republic if the Minister believes, on reasonable grounds, that a thing relevant to the proceeding or investigation may be located in a foreign country.

(2) The Minister may request the appropriate authority of the foreign country to obtain a warrant or other instrument that, under the law of the foreign country, authorises:

(a) a search for a thing relevant to the proceeding or investigation; and

(b) if that thing or any other thing that is or may be relevant to the proceeding or investigation is found as a result of the search, the seizure of that thing.

(3) A thing that:

(a) is relevant to the proceeding or investigation; and

(b) has been obtained by the appropriate authority of the foreign country, by a process authorised by the law of that country other than the issue as requested by the Republic of a warrant or other instrument authorising the seizure of the thing, may be admissible in evidence in the proceeding or used in the investigation despite having been obtained otherwise than in accordance with the request.

#### 18 Request by foreign countries for search and seizure

(1) The Minister may direct an authorised officer to apply to a Judge for a search warrant, if:

(a) a proceeding or investigation for a criminal matter involving a serious offence has commenced in a foreign country;

(b) the Minister believes, on reasonable grounds, that a thing relevant to the investigation or proceeding is located in the Republic; and

(c) the foreign country requests the Minister to arrange for the issue of a search warrant for that thing.

(2) The authorised officer may apply to the court for the issue of a warrant to search land or premises in the Republic for a thing relevant to the proceeding or investigation.

#### 19 Search warrants

(1) Where an application is made under Section 18 for a warrant for a thing relevant to an investigation or proceeding in a foreign country, the court may issue the warrant authorising the authorised officer, with any assistance, and by any force, that is necessary and reasonable:

(a) to enter the land or premises; and

(b) to search the land or premises for that thing and to seize it.

(2) A warrant issued under this Section shall include:

(a) a statement of the purpose for which the warrant is issued, including a reference to the nature of the relevant offence;

(b) a description of the kind of thing authorised to be seized;

(c) a time at which the warrant ceases to have effect; and

(d) a statement as to whether entry is authorised at any time or at specified times.

(3) Where, in the course of searching under a warrant issued under Section 18 for a thing of a kind specified in the warrant, an authorised officer finds another thing, the warrant is taken to authorise the authorised officer to seize the other thing if the officer believes, on reasonable grounds, the other thing:

(a) to be relevant to the proceeding or investigation in the foreign country or to provide evidence about the commission of a criminal offence in the Republic; and

(b) to be likely to be concealed, lost or destroyed if it is not seized.

#### 20 Custody of things seized

(1) Where an authorised officer seizes a thing under this Part, the officer shall deliver the thing into the custody and control of the Commissioner of Police.

(2) Where a thing is delivered into the custody and control of the Commissioner of Police under subsection (1), the Commissioner of Police shall tell the Minister that the thing has been so delivered and arrange for the thing to be kept in safe custody.

(3) The Minister may give to the Commissioner of Police a direction in writing including a direction that the thing be sent to an authority of a foreign country about how the thing shall be dealt with. s 19LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 5 — ARRANGEMENTS FOR PERSONS TO GIVE EVIDENCE OR ASSIST INVESTIGATIONS

#### 21 Requests for removal of certain persons to the Republic

(1) The Minister may request a foreign country to authorise the attendance at a hearing, for a proceeding that has commenced in the Republic, of a person who is in the foreign country if:

(a) the proceeding relates to a criminal matter; and

(b) the Minister is of the opinion that the person:

(i) is a foreign prisoner; and

(ii) is capable of giving evidence relevant to the proceeding; and has consented to being removed to the Republic to give evidence in the proceeding.

(2) The Minister may request a foreign country to authorise removal of a person who is in the foreign country to the Republic to give assistance investigation that has commenced in the Republic if:

(a) the investigation relates to a criminal matter; and

(b) the Minister is of the opinion that the person:

(i) is a foreign prisoner;

(ii) is capable of giving assistance in the investigation; and

(iii) has consented to being removed to the Republic to give assistance in the investigation.

(3) Where the Minister makes a request under subsection (1) or (2), the Minister may arrange with an appropriate authority of the foreign country for:

(a) the removal of the person to the Republic;

(b) the custody of the person while in the Republic;

(c) the return of the person to the foreign country; and

(d) other relevant matters.

#### 22 Custody of certain persons

(1) This Section applies:

(a) to a person who is to be brought to the Republic from a foreign country in response to a request under Section 21; and

(b) if the foreign country requests that the person be kept in custody while he or she is in the Republic.

(2) The person shall be kept in the custody that the Minister directs, in writing, while the person is in the Republic, or travelling to or from the Republic, under the request.

#### 23 Immunities

(1) This Section applies to a person who is in the Republic:

(a) because of a request under Section 21; or

(b) to give evidence in a proceeding or to give assistance in an investigation, because of a request made by the Minister except a request under Section 21, for international assistance in a criminal matter.

(2) The person shall not:

(a) be detained, prosecuted or punished in the Republic for any offence committed, or alleged to have been committed, before he or she left the foreign country in response to the request;

(b) be subjected to any civil suit for any act or omission of the person that occurred, or is alleged to have occurred, before he or she left the foreign country under the request, being a civil suit to which the person could not be subjected if the person were not in the Republic;

(c) be required to give evidence in any proceeding in the Republic other than any proceeding to which the request relates;

(d) be required, in any proceeding to which the request relates, to answer any question that the person would not be required to answer in a proceeding relating to a criminal matter in the foreign country; or

(e) be required, in any proceeding to which the request relates, to produce any document or article that the person would not be required to produce in a proceeding in the foreign country relating to a criminal matter.

(3) A foreign law immunity certificate is admissible in proceedings of the matter stated in the certificate.

(4) Subsection (2) ceases to apply to a person:

(a) when the person leaves the Republic; or

(b) if the person has had the opportunity of leaving the Republic and has remained in the Republic otherwise than:

(i) for the purpose to which the request relates;

(ii) to give evidence in a proceeding in the Republic certified by the Minister, in writing, to be a proceeding in which it is desirable that the person give evidence; or

(iii) to give assistance in an investigation in the Republic certified by the Minister, in writing so be an investigation in which it is desirable that the person give assistance.

(5) A certificate given by the Minister for subsection (4)(b)(ii) or (iii) has effect from the day specified in it (which may be a day before the day on which the certificate is given).

#### 24 Status of person prosecuted for offence committed after

leaving foreign country

(1) This Section applies to a person who has come to the Republic in response to a request under Section 21.

(2) For the purposes of this Act, the person is taken to be in the Republic because of the request during any period when he or she remains in the Republic to be tried for a criminal offence against the law of the Republic that he or she is alleged to have committed after he or she left the foreign country.

(3) Without limiting subsection (2), the person shall be kept in the custody that the Minister directs under Section 22.

#### 27 Release of certain persons on request by foreign country

The Minister shall direct that a person be released from custody if:

(a) the person is kept in custody under a direction of the Minister under Section 22; and

(b) the foreign country from which the person has been brought request the release of the person from custody.

#### 28 Arrest of person who has escaped from custody

(1) A police officer may arrest a person without warrant if the officer believes, on reasonable grounds, that the person:

(a) has been brought to the Republic in response to a request under Section 21; and

(b) has escaped from lawful custody while in the Republic.

(2) The police officer shall take the person before the court as soon as practicable.

(3) Where the court is satisfied that the person has escaped from lawful custody, the court may issue a warrant authorising any police officer to return the person to lawful custody.

## PART 6 — CUSTODY OF PERSONS IN TRANSIT

#### 34 Transit

(1) Subsection (2) applies to a person who is to be given in custody from foreign country through the Republic to another foreign country to give evidence in a proceeding or assistance in an investigation in a criminal matter in the other foreign country.

(2) The person:

(a) may be taken through the Republic in the custody of another person; and

(b) if an aircraft or ship on which the person is a passenger lands or calls at a place in the Republic, shall be kept in custody as the Minister directs, in writing, until the person’s journey is continued.

(3) The Minister may direct that the person be taken in custody to the foreign country from which the person was first taken if:

(a) the person is being held in custody because of a direction under subsection (2)(b); and

(b) the person’s journey is not, in the opinion of the Minister, continued within a reasonable time.

#### 35 Arrest of person in transit

(1) A police officer may arrest a person without warrant if the police officer believes, on reasonable grounds, that the person was being kept in custody under a direction under Section 34(2)(b) and has escaped from that custody.

(2) The police officer shall take the person before the court as soon as practicable.

(3) Where the court is satisfied that the person has escaped from lawful custody, the court may issue a warrant authorising any police officer to return the person to lawful custody.

## PART 7 — ASSISTANCE REGARDING PROCEEDS OF CRIME

#### 36 Requests for enforcement of orders made in the Republic

(1) The Minister may request an appropriate authority of a foreign country to make arrangements for the enforcement of:

(a) a forfeiture order for a serious offence, made in the Republic, against property that is believed to be located in the foreign country;

(b) a pecuniary penalty order for a serious offence, made in the Republic, if some or all of the property available to satisfy the order is believed to be located in the foreign country; or

(c) a restraining order for a serious offence, made in the Republic, against property that is believed to be located in that foreign country.

(2) The Minister may enter an arrangement with the foreign country to share with the country the amount forfeited under subsection (1)(a) or paid under subsection (1)(b).

#### 37 Requests for issue of orders in foreign countries

## PART 9 — ADMISSIBILITY OF FOREIGN EVIDENCE

#### 49 Definitions for Part 9

In this Part:

‘civil proceeding’ means a proceeding other than a criminal proceeding;

‘criminal proceeding’ includes a:

(a) prosecution for an offence; and

(b) proceeding for the committal of a person for trial for an offence; and

(c) proceeding for the sentencing of a person convicted of an offence;

‘foreign law’ means a law of, or in force in, a country other than the Republic;

‘foreign material’ means:

(a) the testimony of a person that:

(i) was obtained as a result of a request for foreign material made under this Part; and

(ii) complies with Section 51;

(b) any exhibit annexed to the testimony; and

(c) any part of the testimony or exhibit; and

‘Naoero court’ means:

(a) the District Court; or

(b) a Resident Magistrate acting under the laws of the Republic;

[def am Act 29 of 2026 s 5, opn 20 July 2026]

‘related civil proceedings’, for a criminal proceeding, means civil proceedings arising from the same subject matter from which the criminal proceeding arose.

#### 50 Application of this Part

This Part applies to:

(a) a proceeding, before a Naoero court, that is:

(i) a criminal proceeding for an offence against the law of the Republic; or

(ii) a related civil proceeding;

(b) testimony obtained as a result of a request made by or for the Minister to a foreign country for the testimony of a person; and

(c) any exhibit annexed to the testimony.

[s 50 am Act 29 of 2026 s 6, opn 20 July 2026]

#### 51 Requirements for testimony from a foreign country

(1) The testimony shall be taken before a court in the foreign country concerned:

(a) on oath or affirmation; or

(b) under any caution or admonition as would be accepted by courts in the foreign country concerned, for giving testimony in proceedings before such courts.

(2) The testimony may be taken in closed court of the foreign country concerned.

[s 51 subst Act 29 of 2026 s 7, opn 20 July 2026]

#### 52 Form of testimony

(1) The testimony may be recorded in any of the following ways:

(a) in writing;

(b) on audio tape; or

(c) on video tape.

(2) The testimony need not:

(a) be in the form of an affidavit; or

(b) constitute a transcript of a proceeding in a foreign court.

(3) The testimony shall be endorsed with, or be accompanied by, a certificate to the effect that it is an accurate record of the evidence given.

(4) The certificate shall:

(a) purport to be signed or certified by a Judge, Resident Magistrate or Judicial officer in or of the foreign country to which the request was made; and

(b) purport to bear an official or public seal of:

(i) the country; or

(ii) an authority of the country responsible for matters relating to justice, that is, a Minister of State, a Ministry or Department of Government, or an officer of the Government.

#### 53 Foreign material may be adduced as evidence

Foreign material may be adduced as evidence in a proceeding to which this Part applies unless:

(a) at the hearing of the proceeding, it appears to the Naoero court’s satisfaction that the person who gave the testimony concerned is present in the Republic and is able to testify at the hearing;

(b) the evidence would not have been admissible if it had been given by the person at the hearing; or

(c) in the proceeding the Naoero court directs, under Section 54, that the foreign material shall not be adduced as evidence.

[s 53 am Act 29 of 2026 s 8, opn 20 July 2026]

#### 54 Direction to prevent foreign material being adduced

(1) The Naoero court may direct that foreign material may not be adduced as evidence if it appears to the Naoero court’s satisfaction that the interests of justice would be better served if the foreign material were not adduced as evidence.

[subs (1) am Act 29 of 2026 s 8, opn 20 July 2026]

(a) the extent to which the foreign material provides evidence that would not otherwise be available;

(b) the probative value of the foreign material for any issue that is likely to be determined in the proceedings;

(c) the extent to which statements contained in the foreign material could, at the time they were made, be challenged by questioning the persons who made there;

(d) whether exclusion of the foreign material would cause undue expense or delay; and s 52LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

(i) the defence in criminal proceedings; or

(ii) a party to related civil proceeding.

(1) The Minister may certify that specified foreign material was obtained as a result of a request made to a foreign country by the Minister.

#### 56 Effect of this Part on right to examine witnesses

(1) Nothing in this Part limits a right of the defendant in criminal proceedings to which this Part applies to examine, in person or by his or her legal representative, a witness whose testimony is admitted in evidence in those proceedings.

(2) Where the defendant requests that the person who gave the testimony be present at the hearing in the Republic to be cross-examined, the Naoero court shall warn the defendant that he or she may be ordered to pay any expenses incurred by the State in making the person available before the Naoero court.

[subs (2) am Act 29 of 2026 s 10, opn 20 July 2026]

(a) as a result of the request, the person appears before the Naoero court as a witness; and

[subs (3) am Act 29 of 2026 s 10, opn 20 July 2026]

## PART 10 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 58 Delegation

The Minister may delegate to the Secretary for Justice all or any of his or her powers under this Act.

#### 59 Authentication of document

(1) In a proceeding under this Act or an order under the Proceeds of Crime Act arising directly or indirectly from a request made under this Act, any document that is authenticated is admissible in evidence.

(2) A document is authenticated if it purports to be:

(a) signed or certified by a Judge, Resident Magistrate or Judicial officer in or of a foreign country; and

(b) sealed with an official or public seal of the foreign country or of a Minister of State, or of a Ministry or Department of the Government or an officer of the Government, of the foreign country.

(3) Nothing in this Section prevents the proof of any matter or the admission in evidence of any document under any other law.

#### 60 Restriction on use of information, etc

(1) A material, whether it is evidence, a document, an article or a thing, that is sent to the Republic by a foreign country:

(a) because of a request made by the Minister under this Act; and

(b) for a proceeding or investigation in a criminal matter, shall not be used intentionally for any other purpose without the approval of the Minister.

(2) The material is inadmissible in evidence in any proceeding other than the proceeding for which it was obtained unless the Minister approves its use for that other proceeding.

(3) Any information, document, article or thing obtained directly or indirectly from a person by making use of the material:

(a) otherwise than for the proceeding or investigation for which it was obtained; and

(b) without the approval of the Minister, is inadmissible in evidence in any other proceeding and may not be used for any other investigation.

(4) A person who contravenes subsection (1) or (3), is guilty of an offence punishable by:

(a) if the person is a natural person, a fine of up to $10,000 or a term of imprisonment up to 2 years or to both; or

(b) if the person is body corporate, a fine of up to $50,000.

(5) For the purposes of this Section, disclosure of any material is taken to be a use of that material.

#### 61 Requests for international assistance shall not be disclosed

(1) Subsection (2) applies to a person who, because of his or her office or employment, has knowledge of the:

(a) contents of a request for international assistance made by a foreign country to the Republic under this Act;

(b) fact that a request has been made; or

(c) fact that a request has been granted or refused.

(2) The person shall not intentionally disclose those contents or that fact unless:

(a) it is necessary to do so in the performance of his or her duties; or

(b) the Minister has given his or her approval to the disclosure of those contents or that fact.

(3) A person who contravenes subsection (2), is guilty of an offence punishable by:

(a) if the person is a natural person, a fine of up to $10,000 or a term of imprisonment of up to 2 years or to both; or

(b) if the person is a body corporate, a fine of up to $50,000.

#### 62 Regulations

The Cabinet may, from time to time, make regulations for all or any of the following purposes:

(a) prescribing the forms of applications, notices, certificates and other documents for the purposes of this Act;

(b) prescribing the procedure to be followed in dealing with requests made pursuant to this Act, and providing for notification of the results of action taken pursuant to any request;

(c) prescribing the procedures for obtaining evidence, or producing documents pursuant to a request made under Section 11;

(d) providing for the payment of fees, travelling allowances, and expenses to any person in the Republic who gives or produces evidence or assistance pursuant to a request made under this Act;

(e) prescribing conditions for the protection of any property sent to or by a foreign country pursuant to a request made under this Act; and making provision for the return of the property to the Republic pursuant to a request; and

(f) providing for any other matters that are contemplated by or are necessary for giving full effect to this Act and for its administration.
