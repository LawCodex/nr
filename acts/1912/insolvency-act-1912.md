# Insolvency Act 1912

## Table of Amendments

The Insolvency Act 1912 No 48 was made on 16 July 1912 and commenced on 23 December 1912 (Papua Government Gazette 23 December 1912). This Act applied in the Republic from 23 September 1922 (Laws Repeal and Adopting Act 1922, s 14 and Sch 3).

An Act to provide for the distribution of the estates of insolvent debtors amongst their creditors, and their release from their debts, and for other purposes.

Be it enacted by the Lieutenant-Governor of the Territory of Papua with the advice and consent of the Legislative Council and is adopted by the Republic:

## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title and commencement

This Act may be cited as the Insolvency Act 1912 and came into effect on 23 December 1912.

[s 1 subst Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[77], opn 15 Apr 2011]

[s 2 rep Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[77], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 3 Interpretation

In this Act:

‘Court’ means the Supreme Court established under the Constitution and the Supreme Court Act 2018;

[def am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968]

‘creditor’ means also any 2 or more persons being jointly interested as partners or otherwise and incorporated and joint stock companies;

‘creditors present at any meeting’ and ‘creditors assembled at any meeting’ include creditors who are represented by some person duly authorised by such creditors in writing;

‘debt provable in insolvency’ includes any debt or liability by this Act made provable in insolvency;

‘Examining Magistrate’ means any Resident Magistrate before whom a person is ordered to attend for examination under this Act;

‘property’ means and includes money goods things in action land and every description of property whether real or personal also obligations easements and every description of estate interest and profit present or future vested or contingent arising out of or incident to property as above defined;

‘Registrar’ means the Registrar of Courts;

[def am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968]

‘Registry’ means the Registry of Courts; and

‘secured creditor’ means any creditor holding any mortgage charge or lien on the insolvent’s estate or any part of it as security for a debt due to him or her.

[s 3 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 4 Mode of calculating majority of creditors

Whenever under this Act it shall be necessary to compute a majority of creditors no creditor whose debt does not exceed $10 shall be counted in reckoning a majority in number but the debt due to such creditor shall be taken into account in reckoning a majority in value.

[s 4 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

## PART 2 — CONSTITUTION AND POWERS OF COURT – PRACTICE

#### 7 Supreme Court to be Court of insolvency

(1) The Supreme Court shall be the Court of insolvency.

(2) All the powers under this Act conferred upon the said Court may be exercised by a single Judge.

[s 7 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968; Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 8 Judge may sit in chambers

A Judge may sit in chambers for the despatch of such business as may without detriment to the public advantage arising from the discussion of questions in open court be heard in chambers and when sitting in chambers he or she shall have in all respects like power and jurisdiction as when sitting in Court.

#### 9 Powers of Judge in insolvency

Every Judge in insolvency shall for the purposes of this Act have all the powers and jurisdiction of a Judge of the Supreme Court and the orders of such Judge may be enforced accordingly in the same manner as those of a Judge of the Supreme Court or in such other manner as may be prescribed.

[s 9 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968; Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 10 Court may review its orders

The Court may review, rescind or vary any order made by it in pursuance of this Act.

[s 10 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 11 Special case may be stated

In any insolvency or other proceeding within the jurisdiction of the Court the parties concerned may at any stage of the proceedings state any question or questions in a special case for the opinion of the Court and if in such case it is stated that the decision of the Court shall be final, no appeal shall lie therefrom.

#### 12 Mode of taking evidence

The Court may in all matters within its jurisdiction at its discretion take the whole or any part of the evidence vivâ voce upon oath or by interrogatories in writing or upon affidavit or in any of such ways and may issue commissions for the purpose of taking evidence at any place either within or outside the Republic.

#### 13 Court may award costs

The Court may award costs in any matters under this Act and costs so awarded shall be recoverable in the same manner as costs awarded by an order of the Supreme Court and the like remedies may be had on an order under this Act for costs as on any such order of the Supreme Court or such other remedies as may be prescribed.

[s 13 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968; Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 14 General power of the Court

Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Court shall have full power to decide all questions of priorities and all other questions whatsoever whether of law or fact arising in any case of insolvency coming within the cognizance of the court or which the court may deem it expedient or necessary to decide for the purpose of doing complete justice or making a complete distribution of property in any such case.

[s 14 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 15 Commitment to prison

Where the Court commits a person to prison, the commitment may be to such convenient prison as the court thinks expedient and if the Chief Correctional Officer of the Correctional Centre refuses to receive any prisoner so committed he or she shall be liable for every such refusal to a penalty not exceeding $100.

GENERAL RULES

#### 16 General rules may be made from time to time

(1) General rules in this Act described as rules of the Court may be made from time to time for the effectual execution of this Act and of the objects of it and the regulation of the practice and procedure upon insolvency petitions and the proceedings thereon and the said general rules may from time to time be revoked and altered.

(2) All such rules shall be made by the Chief Justice.

[s 16 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 17 Matters to be provided for by general rules

Any general rules made may prescribe regulations:

(a) as to the service of insolvency petitions including provisions for substituted service;

(b) as to the valuing of any debts provable in an insolvency;

(c) as to the valuation of securities held by creditors;

(d) as to the giving or withholding interest or discount on or in respect of debts or dividends;

(e) as to the funds out of which costs are to be paid the order of payment and the amount of taxation;

(f) as to the fees to be charged and collected for any business done by the Court of insolvency or any officer of it and as to whether the same shall be collected by stamps or otherwise; and

(g) as to any other matter or thing whether similar or not to those above enumerated in respect to which it may be expedient to make rules for carrying into effect the objects of this Act, and any rules so made shall be deemed to be within the powers conferred by this Act and shall be of the same force as if they were enacted in this Act.

[s 17 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 18 Continuance of rules in force

Until rules have been made under this Act and so far as such rules do not extend s 14LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO the principles, practice and rules on which the Supreme Court has heretofore acted in dealing with insolvency proceedings shall be observed by the Court in insolvency cases under this Act.

[s 18 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968; Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

## PART 3 — DEBTOR’S PETITION

#### 25 Debtor’s petition

A debtor may petition for adjudication of insolvency against himself or herself and the filing of such petition shall be an act of insolvency.

#### 26 Form of debtor’s petition

Debtors’ petitions may be in the following form or to the like effect:

To the Registrar of Courts The Petition of A.B. of showeth that your petitioner is unable to meet his or her engagements with his or her creditors.

Your petitioner prays that he or she be adjudicated insolvent, and shall be signed by the petitioner and attested by some justice of the peace or commissioner for oaths or the Registrar.

[s 26 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968]

#### 27 Petitions by partnerships

A petition for insolvency against a partnership may be presented by the majority of the members of it who at the time of presenting the petition are usually resident within the Republic.

#### 28 Adjudication on petition

When a debtor’s petition shall have been presented the Court shall forthwith after such presentation proceed to adjudicate thereon and upon due proof make adjudication of insolvency against the petitioner.

## PART 4 — CREDITOR’S PETITION

#### 29 Acts of insolvency

Any debtor liable to the provisions of this Act who shall have committed any of the following acts shall be deemed to have committed an act of insolvency and shall be liable to be adjudicated insolvent upon the petition of any creditor or creditors competent to present such petition that is to say:

(a) if the debtor has in the Republic or elsewhere made a conveyance or assignment of his or her property to a trustee or trustees for the benefit of his or her creditors generally;

(b) if the debtor has in the Republic or elsewhere made a fraudulent conveyance gift delivery or transfer of his or her property in whole or in part;

(c) if the debtor has with intent to defeat or delay his or her creditors done any of the following things namely, departed out of the Republic or being out of the Republic, remained out of the Republic or departed from his or her dwelling-house or otherwise absented himself or herself or begun to keep house;

(d) if the debtor has filed in the prescribed manner a declaration admitting his or her inability to pay his or her debts;

(e) if the debtor has presented a petition for adjudication of insolvency against himself or herself under Part 3;

(f) if an execution issued against a debtor on any legal process for the purpose of obtaining payment of not less than $50 has been levied by seizure and the debtor has not bona fide satisfied such process by payment or otherwise within 4 days from such seizure;

(g) if the debtor has after the presentation of a petition for adjudication of insolvency against him or her paid, given or delivered or caused to be paid given or delivered to the creditor or creditors presenting such petition money or any satisfaction as security for his or her or their debt or debts or any part, whereby the petitioning creditor or creditors may receive more in the dollar than other creditors;

(h) if the debtor has given or executed any fraudulent warrant of attorney or cognovit actionem;

(i) if the creditor presenting the petition has served on the debtor in the prescribed manner a debtor’s summons requiring him or her to pay a sum due of an amount of not less than $50 and the debtor has for such time succeeding the service of the summons as may be specified therein neglected to pay such sum or to secure or compound for the same to the satisfaction of the creditor;

(j) if a debtor having against him or her the sentence, judgment or decree of any competent court and being thereunto required has failed to satisfy the same or to point out to the officer charged with the execution for sufficient disposable of property to satisfy the debt;

(k) if at any meeting of his or her creditors a debtor shall have consented to present a petition under Part 3 and shall not within 48 hours from the time of such consenting, or such further time as may be rendered necessary by illness, distance or other sufficient cause, have presented such petition;

(l) if at any meeting of his or her creditors a debtor shall have admitted that he or she is unable to meet his or her engagements or shall offer a composition of less than 20 cents in a dollar in cash and having been requested by a majority of the creditors present at such meeting to present a petition under Part 3 shall not within 48 hours after such request, or such further time as may be rendered necessary by illness distance or other sufficient cause, have presented such petition;

(m) if a debtor has given or made any preference to or in favour of any creditor which if the debtor were adjudicated insolvent under this Act would be deemed a fraudulent preference of such creditor; or

(n) if a debtor has been adjudged or declared bankrupt or insolvent by a court of a Commonwealth country having jurisdiction in bankruptcy or insolvency or for the relief of insolvent debtors or has presented a petition to any such court praying adjudication of bankruptcy or insolvency against himself or herself.

[s 29 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 30 Act of insolvency shall be committed within 6 months prior to

presentation of the petition No person shall be adjudged an insolvent on any of the grounds in Section 29 unless the act of insolvency on which the adjudication is grounded has occurred within 6 months before the presentation of the petition for adjudication.

#### 31 Amount of petitioning creditor’s debt

(1) A single creditor if the debt due to him or her by a debtor amounts to $50 or upwards or 2 creditors not being partners if the aggregate amount of debts due to them by a debtor amounts to $70 or upwards or 3 or more creditors not being partners if the aggregate amount of debts due to them by a debtor amounts to $100 or upwards may present a petition to the court praying that the debtor be adjudged an insolvent and alleging as the ground for such adjudication any one or more of the acts or defaults defined to be acts of insolvency.

(2) Any number of creditors in partnership shall for the purposes of this Section be deemed to be a single creditor.

#### 32 Nature of petitioning creditor’s debt

(1) The debt of the petitioning creditor shall be a liquidated sum due at law or in equity and subsisting at the time when the act of insolvency was committed at the time of presenting the petition.

(2) It shall not be a secured debt unless the petitioner states in his or her petition that he or she will give up such security for the benefit of the creditors in the event of the debtor being adjudicated insolvent or unless the petitioner gives an estimate of the value of his or her security in which latter case, he or she may be admitted as a petitioning creditor to the extent of the balance of the debt due to him or her after deducting the value so estimated.

(3) A person under subsection (2) shall on an application being made by the trustee within the prescribed time after the date of adjudication give up his or her security to such trustee for the benefit of the creditors upon payment of such estimated value. s 29LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

(4) For a person who has given credit to a debtor for valuable consideration for any sum payable at a certain time which time had not elapsed when the act of insolvency was committed may petition or join in petitioning whether the time for payment has arrived at the date of presenting the petition or not.

#### 33 Proceedings in relation to a debtor’s summons

(1) A debtor’s summons may be granted by a Judge of the Supreme Court on a creditor proving to his or her satisfaction that a debt sufficient to support a petition in insolvency is due to the creditor from the person against whom the summons is sought and that the creditor has failed to obtain payment of his or her debt after using reasonable efforts to do so.

(2) The summons shall be in the prescribed form resembling as nearly as circumstances admit a writ issued by the Supreme Court.

(3) It shall state that in the event of the debtor failing to pay the sum specified in the summons or to compound for the same to the satisfaction of the creditor a petition may be presented against him or her praying that he or she may be adjudged an insolvent.

(4) The summons shall have an indorsement thereon to the like effect or such other prescribed indorsement as may be best calculated to indicate to the debtor the nature of the document served upon him or her and the consequences of inattention to the requisitions made.

[s 33 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968]

#### 34 Debtor may apply to dismiss summons

(1) A debtor served with a debtor’s summons may apply to a Judge of the Supreme Court in the prescribed manner and within the prescribed time to dismiss such summons on the ground that he or she is not indebted to the creditor serving such summons or that he or she is not indebted to such amount as will justify such creditor in presenting an insolvency petition against him or her and such Judge may dismiss the summons with or without costs if satisfied with the allegations made by the debtor or it may upon such security, if any, being given as may seem just for payment to the creditor of the debt alleged by him or her to be due and the costs of establishing such debt stay all proceedings on the summons for such time as will be required for the trial of the question relating to such debt.

(2) Such trial shall be had either before the Supreme Court or a Judge or some other court by law competent to try and determine questions relating to debts of a like amount.

[s 34 am GN No 188/1969 O 3, opn 31 Jan 1968]

In petitions the following short statements of acts of insolvency may be used and if such or the like statements are used they shall be sufficient:

(b) made a fraudulent conveyance or gift or delivery or transfer of his or her property or part of his or her property to wit to E.F.;

(c) with intent to defeat or delay his or her creditors departed out of the Republic or remained out of the Republic or etc;

(d) filed a declaration of insolvency;

(e) filed an insolvency petition;

(f) suffered his or her property to be seized in execution for $50 and upwards and detained for 4 days;

(h) gave a fraudulent warrant of attorney or cognovit actionem;

(i) failed for days to pay secure or compound a debt of $ which he or she was required to pay by a debtor’s summons;

(j) failed to satisfy or point out property to satisfy an execution;

(k) at a creditors’ meeting consented to present an insolvency petition and failed to do so for 48 hours without excuse;

(n) was adjudged insolvent or bankrupt or presented a petition or as the case may be for adjudication of insolvency or bankruptcy against himself or herself.

#### 37 Verification of petition

(1) A petition shall be verified in Court by the oath or affirmation of the petitioner or petitioners.

(2) Where the petitioning creditor is a body corporate or joint stock company or company authorised to sue in the name of a public officer, the petition may be verified by the manager, secretary or other authorised officer of such body corporate or company.

(3) Where the petitioning creditors are 2 or more persons in partnership, the petition may be verified by one only of the partners.

(4) Where the petitioning creditor is out of the Republic the petition may be verified by his or her attorney or agent within the Republic duly authorised on his or her behalf.

#### 38 Signature of petition

on petition of another creditor Where proceedings are stayed under Section 48, the Court may if by reason of the delay caused by such stay of proceedings or for any other cause it thinks just adjudge the debtor an insolvent on the petition of some other creditor and shall thereupon dismiss upon such terms as it thinks just the petition proceedings in which have been stayed.

forfeit his or her debt and pay it to the trustee Where any debtor after the presentation of a petition for adjudication of insolvency against him or her shall pay money to the petitioning creditor or give or deliver or procure to be given or delivered to him or her any satisfaction or security for his or her debt whereby such petitioning creditor may receive more in the dollar in respect of his or her debt than the other creditors such petitioning creditor shall forfeit his or her whole debt and shall also repay or deliver up such money or satisfaction or security or the full value to the trustee.

#### 51 Petitioning creditor to pay costs of proceedings

#### 52 Petitions against partnerships

(1) A creditor of a partnership may present a petition for adjudication of insolvency against all or any one or more of the members of the partnership, if any one of the partners has committed an act of insolvency in respect of the estate of the partnership.

(2) An order of adjudication made under subsection (1) shall be valid although it does not include all the partners.

(3) Following the order in subsection (2), the like proceedings shall be had and taken and concerning such estate and such partner or partners as are provided to be had and taken concerning other estates and persons.

(4) Notwithstanding subsections (1), (2) and (3), no order shall extend or be construed to prevent any creditor of any partnership from proceeding against any partner or the separate estate of any partner of it in respect of debts due by the partnership in the same way as he or she might proceed against him or her in respect of debts due by such partner in his or her individual capacity.

#### 53 Time for delivery of statement of debts, etc, to trustee

An order of adjudication made under Parts 3 and 4 shall specify a time and place at which the debtor is to come in and deliver to the trustee a full true and accurate statement verified on oath of his or her debts and liabilities of every kind and of the names and residences so far as known of his or her creditors and of the causes of his or her inability to meet his or her engagements.

#### 54 Advertisement of adjudication; order conclusive evidence

(1) A copy of the order of the Court adjudging the debtor to be insolvent shall be published in the Gazette and be advertised locally in such manner, if any, as may be prescribed and the date of such order shall be the date of the adjudication for the purposes of this Act.

The Court may by warrant addressed to any constable or prescribed officer of the court cause a debtor to be arrested and any books, papers, monies, goods and chattels in his or her possession to be seized and him or her and them to be safely kept as prescribed until such time as the Court may order under the following circumstances:

(1) Demands in the nature of unliquidated damages arising otherwise than by reason of a contract or promise shall not be provable in insolvency and no person having notice of any act of insolvency available for adjudication against the insolvent shall prove for any debt or liability contracted by the insolvent subsequently to the date of his or her so having notice.

#### 160 Supplemental regulations as to proceeding in insolvency

The following provisions shall apply to proceedings in insolvency namely:

(a) where 2 or more insolvency petitions are presented against the same debtor or against debtors being members of the same partnership the Court may consolidate the proceedings or any of them upon such terms as the Court thinks fit;

## PART 9 — LIQUIDATION BY ARRANGEMENT REGULATIONS

#### 183 Regulations as to liquidation by arrangement

The following provisions shall be made with respect to the liquidation by arrangement of the affairs of a debtor:

(a) a debtor unable to pay his or her debts may summon a general meeting of his or her creditors and such meeting may by a special resolution as defined by this Act declare that the affairs of the debtor are to be liquidated by arrangement and not in insolvency and may at that or some subsequent meeting held at an interval of not more than a week appoint a trustee with or without a committee of inspection;

(b) all the provisions of this Act relating to a first meeting of creditors and to subsequent meetings of creditors in the case of an insolvency including the description of creditors entitled to vote at such meetings and the debts in respect of which they are entitled to vote shall apply respectively to the first meeting of creditors and to subsequent meetings of creditors for the purposes of this Section subject to the following modifications:

(i) that every such meeting shall be presided over by such chairman as the meeting may elect; and

(ii) that no creditor shall be entitled to vote until he or she has proved by a statutory declaration a debt provable in insolvency to be due to him or her and the amount of such debt with any prescribed particulars and a person wilfully making a false declaration in relation to such debt shall be guilty of a misdemeanor;

(c) the debtor unless prevented by sickness or other cause satisfactory to such meeting shall be present at the meeting at which the special resolution is passed and shall answer any inquiries made of him or her and he or she or if he or she is so prevented from being at such meeting someone on his or her behalf shall produce to the meeting a statement showing the whole of his or her assets and debts and the names and addresses of the creditors to whom his or her debts are due;

(d) the special resolution together with the statement of the assets and debts of the debtor and the name of the trustee appointed and of the members, if any, of the committee of inspection shall be presented to the Registrar and it shall be his or her duty to inquire whether such resolution has been passed in manner directed by this Section but if satisfied that it was so passed and that a trustee has been appointed with or without a committee of inspection he or she shall forthwith register the resolution and the statement of the assets and debts of the debtor and such resolution and statement shall be open for inspection on the prescribed conditions and the liquidation by arrangement shall be deemed to have commenced as from the date of the appointment of the trustee;

(e) all such property of the debtor as would if he or she were made insolvent be divisible among his or her creditors shall from and after the date of the appointment of a trustee vest in such trustee under a liquidation by arrangement and be divisible amongst the creditors and all such settlements, conveyances, transfers, charges, payments, obligations and proceedings as would be void against the trustee in the case of an insolvency shall be void against the trustee in the case of liquidation by arrangement;

(f) the certificate of the Registrar in respect of the appointment of any trustee in the case of a liquidation by arrangement shall be of the same effect as a certificate of the Court to the like effect in the case of an insolvency;

(g) the trustee under a liquidation shall have the same powers and perform the same duties as a trustee under an insolvency and the property of the debtor shall be distributed in the same manner as in an insolvency and with the modification hereinafter mentioned all the provisions of this Act shall so far as the same are applicable apply to the case of a liquidation by arrangement in the same manner as if the word ‘insolvent’ included a debtor whose affairs are under liquidation and the word ‘insolvency’ included liquidation by arrangement and in construing such provisions the appointment of a trustee under a liquidation shall according to circumstances be deemed to be equivalent to and a substitute for the presentation of a petition in insolvency or the service of such petition or an order of adjudication in insolvency;

(h) the creditors at their first or any general meeting may prescribe the bank into which the trustee is to pay any monies received by him or her and the sum which he or she may retain in his or her hands;

(i) the provisions of this Act with respect to the close of the insolvency discharge of an insolvent to the release of the trustee shall not apply in the case of a debtor whose affairs are under liquidation by arrangement but the close of the liquidation may be fixed and the discharge of the debtor and the release of the trustee may be granted by a special resolution of the creditors in general meeting:

Provided that no trustee shall be released unless his or her accounts have been audited in the manner provided in paragraph (j);

(k) the trustee shall report to the Registrar the discharge of the debtor and a certificate of such discharge given by the Registrar shall have the same effect as a certificate of discharge given to an insolvent under this Act;

(l) rules of court may be made in relation to proceedings on the occasion of liquidation by arrangement in the same manner and to the same extent and of the same authority as in respect of proceedings in insolvency;

(m) if it appears to the Court on satisfactory evidence that the liquidation by arrangement cannot in consequence of legal difficulties or of there being no trustee for the time being or for any sufficient cause proceed without injustice or undue delay to the creditors or to the debtor the Court may on the petition of the debtor or of any creditor competent under this Act to present a petition for adjudication adjudge the debtor an insolvent and proceedings may be had accordingly; and

(n) where no committee of inspection is appointed the trustee may act on his or her own discretion in cases where he or she would otherwise have been bound to refer to such committee.

## PART 10 — COMPOSITION WITH CREDITORS

#### 185 Regulations as to composition by creditors

(1) The creditors of a debtor unable to pay his or her debts may without any proceedings in insolvency by an extraordinary resolution resolve that a composition shall be accepted in satisfaction of the debts due to them from the debtor.

(2) An extraordinary resolution of creditors shall be a resolution which has been passed by a majority in number and three-fourths in value of the creditors of the debtor assembled at a general meeting to be held in the manner prescribed of which notice has been given in the prescribed manner and has been confirmed by a majority in number and value of the creditors assembled at a subsequent general meeting of which notice has been given in the prescribed manner and held at an interval of not less than 7 days nor more than 14 days from the date of the meeting at which such resolution was first passed.

(3) In calculating a majority for the purposes of a composition under this Section, the value of the debts of secured creditors shall as nearly as circumstances admit be estimated in the same way and the same description of creditors shall be entitled to vote at such general meetings as in insolvency.

(4) The debtor unless prevented by sickness or other cause satisfactory to such meetings shall be present at both the meetings at which the extraordinary resolution is passed and shall answer any inquiries made of him or her and he or she or if he or she is so prevented from being at such meetings someone on his or her behalf shall produce to the meeting a statement showing the whole of his or her assets and debts and the names and addresses of the creditors to whom such debts respectively are due.

(5) The extraordinary resolution together with the statement of the debtor as to his or her assets and debts shall be presented to the Registrar and it shall be his or her duty to inquire whether such resolution has been passed in manner directed by this Section and if satisfied that it has been so passed he or she shall forthwith register the resolution and statement of assets and debts but until such registration has taken place, such resolution shall be of no validity and any creditor of the debtor may inspect such statement at prescribed times and on payment of such fee if any as may be prescribed.

(6) Before any such registration shall be made the debtor shall make oath that he or she has fully and truly to the best of his or her knowledge disclosed all his or her assets and liabilities and the names of his or her creditors and the amount of their debts and that he or she has not granted or promised any payment or security or made or promised any preference of any creditor or entered into any collusive agreement for the purpose of obtaining assent to the resolution and such oath shall be presented to the Registrar and be registered with the resolution.

(7) The creditors may by an extraordinary resolution add to or vary the provisions of any composition previously accepted by them without prejudice to a persons taking interests under such provisions who do not assent to such addition or variation and any such extraordinary resolution shall be presented to the Registrar in the same manner and with the same consequences as the extraordinary resolution by which the composition was accepted in the first instance.

(8) The provisions of a composition accepted by an extraordinary resolution in pursuance of this Section shall be binding on all the creditors whose names and addresses and the amount of the debts due to whom are shown in the statement of the debtor produced to the meetings at which the resolution has passed but shall not affect or prejudice the rights of any other creditors.

(9) Where a debt arises on a bill of exchange or promissory note if the debtor is ignorant of the holder of any such bill of exchange or promissory note he or she shall be required to state the amount of such bill or note the date on which it falls due the name of the acceptor or person to whom it is payable and any other particulars within his or her knowledge respecting the same and the insertion of such particulars shall be deemed a sufficient description of the creditor of the debtor in respect of such debt and any mistake made inadvertently by a debtor in the statement of his or her debts may be corrected after the prescribed notice has been given with the consent of a general meeting of his or her creditors.

(10) The provisions of any composition made in pursuance of this Section may be enforced by the Court on a motion made in a summary manner by a person interested and any disobedience of the order of the Court made on such motion shall be deemed to be a contempt of court.

(11) Rules of court may be made in relation to proceedings on the occasion of the acceptance of a composition by an extraordinary resolution of creditors in the same manner and to the same extent and of the same authority as in respect of proceedings in insolvency.

(12) Where it appears to the Court on satisfactory evidence that a composition under this Section cannot in consequence of legal difficulties or for any sufficient cause proceed without injustice or undue delay to the creditors or to the debtor the Court may adjudge the debtor an insolvent and proceedings may be had accordingly.

#### 186 Registration of resolution conclusive

The registration by the Registrar of an extraordinary resolution of the creditors under this Part shall in the absence of fraud be conclusive evidence that such resolution was duly passed and all the requisitions of this Act in respect of it complied with but any such registration may nevertheless be set aside or discharged by the Court upon the application of any creditor upon sufficient cause shown.

## PART 11 — OFFENCES

#### 187 Debts incurred by fraud

Where a debtor makes any arrangement or composition with his or her creditors under the provisions of this Act he or she shall remain liable for the unpaid balance of any debt which he or she incurred or increased or whereof before the date of the arrangement or composition he or she obtained forbearance by any fraud provided the defrauded creditor has not assented to the arrangement or composition otherwise than by proving his or her debt and accepting dividends.

[s 187 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 188 Order by Court for prosecution on report of trustee

Where a trustee in any insolvency reports to the Court that in his or her opinion an insolvent has been guilty of any offence under this Act or under any of the provisions of Division 9.3 of the Crimes Act 2016 or where it is represented to the Court by any creditor or member of the committee of inspection that there is ground to believe that the insolvent has been guilty of any offence under this Act or under any of the said provisions of the Crimes Act 2016, the Court shall if it appears to the Court that such report or representation is true and that there is a reasonable probability that the insolvent may be convicted order the trustee to prosecute the insolvent for such offence and may hold the insolvent to bail to appear before a justice of the peace to answer the charge to be preferred against him or her for such offence.

[s 188 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011; Act 18 of 2016 s 287 and Sch 2 items 69, 70, opn 12 May 2016]

#### 189 When prosecution ordered by Court expense to be borne by

the Republic; when prosecution not ordered

(1) Where the prosecution of an insolvent under this Act is ordered by the Court or where the insolvent is committed for trial by order of the Court then on the production of the order of such Court all the expenses of the prosecution shall be paid and borne by the Republic.

[subs (1) am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

(2) In other cases, the expenses of the prosecution of an insolvent for an offence under this Act up to the time of his or her commitment for trial shall be paid and borne by the person or persons instituting such prosecution.

[subs (2) am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]

#### 190 Punishments under this Act not cumulative

Where a person is liable under any other written law to any punishment or penalty for any offence made punishable by this Act such person may be proceeded against under any other written law or under this Act so that he or she be not punished twice for the same offence.

[s 190 am Act 8 of 2011 s 12 and Sch 1[78], opn 15 Apr 2011]
