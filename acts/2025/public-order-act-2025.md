# Public Order Act 2025

An Act to provide for public order summary offences and for other related purposes.

Enacted by the Parliament of Nauru as follows:

## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Public Order Act 2025.

#### 2 Commencement

This Act commences on the date it is certified by the Speaker.

#### 3 Objectives of this Act

The objectives of this Act are to provide for the:

(a) specific offences relating to public order, nuisance and related offences;

(b) penalties for offences relating to public order, nuisance and related offences;

(c) procedures to be followed in regulating and maintaining order and prevention of nuisance and related conduct; and

(d) applicability of the Nauru Police Force Act 2025 particularly that the functions and powers of a police officer under that Act that may be performed or exercised for the purposes of enforcing this Act.

#### 4 Interpretation

In this Act:

‘exempt activity’ means an activity that is prescribed by regulations for the purposes of Section 15;

[def insrt Act 14 of 2026 s 4, opn 31 Mar 2026]

‘Minister’ means the Minister responsible for this Act;

[def insrt Act 14 of 2026 s 4, opn 31 Mar 2026]

‘Noise Abatement Direction’ or ‘Direction’ means a direction issued in accordance with Part 3;

‘noise nuisance’ means noise or sound which disrupts activities, causes discomfort or negatively impacts the peace and comfort of a person;

‘police officer’ has the meaning given to it under the Nauru Police Force Act 2025; and

‘public place’ means a place, whether or not covered by water, or premises that is open to the public, or is used by the public, whether or not:

(a) payment of money or other consideration is required to use the place or premises;

(b) the place or premises is ordinarily open or used by the public; or

(c) the public to whom it is open consists only of a class of people.

## PART 2 — PUBLIC ORDER OFFENCES

#### 5 Offences relating to public order and interest

(1) A person shall not:

(a) use any profane, indecent or obscene language in a public place or within hearing of a person in a public place;

(b) disturb a congregation assembled for public worship, or a public meeting or audience at an entertainment or lecture;

(c) disturb a gathering for any purpose including any official meetings;

(d) deface or remove a notice of a public nature exhibited by a person having authority to do so;

(e) use any threatening, abusive, insulting words or behaviour with intent to provoke or cause a breach of the peace;

(f) throw or discharge a stone or other thing at a person, vehicle or building, whether or not the stone or thing actually hits the person or object at which it is aimed; or

(g) hit or throw a ball or other like object used in playing a sport which causes damage, personal injury, or annoyance to a motor vehicle, or person.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $500 for a first offence or an imprisonment term not exceeding 12 months, or both; and

(b) more than $500 but not exceeding $2,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 2 years, for a subsequent offence.

#### 6 Consuming alcohol or being drunk in a public place

(1) A person shall not consume alcohol in a public place.

(2) A person shall not be drunk in a public place.

(3) A person who contravenes subsection (1) or (2) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $500 for a first offence or an imprisonment term not exceeding 12 months, or both; and

(b) more than $500 but not exceeding $2,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 2 years, for a subsequent offence.

#### 7 Drunk and Disorderly in a public place

(1) A person shall not be drunk and disorderly in a public place.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $1,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $1,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years, for a subsequent offence.

#### 8 Noise nuisance in a public place

(1) A person shall not within the hearing of another person in a public place, cause or engage in conduct which causes noise nuisance.

(2) A police officer may immediately direct a person to discontinue conduct in breach of subsection (1).

(3) A police officer may seize any object or equipment used to cause or engage in conduct in breach of subsection (1), where a direction under subsection (2) is not complied with.

(4) A person may be charged with an offence against this Section despite the absence of a complaint by another person.

(5) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $1,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $1,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years, for a subsequent offence.

#### 9 Noise nuisance in a private place

(1) A person may make a complaint to a police officer regarding the conduct of noise nuisance in a private place.

(2) A police officer shall attend a private place upon receiving a complaint of noise nuisance and direct the occupant of such private place to reduce or discontinue the noise nuisance.

(3) Where the noise nuisance is not reduced or discontinued, the police officer may issue a Noise Abatement Direction.

(4) Where the Noise Abatement Direction is not complied with, a police officer may for the purpose of seizing the object or equipment causing the noise nuisance, enter the private premises:

(a) with the occupier’s consent;

(b) with a warrant where the occupier does not consent; or

(c) without a warrant, where the noise nuisance poses an immediate risk to public order or safety.

(5) A person who contravenes a Noise Abatement Direction issued under subsection (3), commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $1,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $1,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years, for a subsequent offence.

[subs (5) am Act 14 of 2026 s 5, opn 31 Mar 2026]

#### 10 Publication of false notices

(1) A person shall not publish in any form or manner, information that is false.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $500 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 12 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $500 but not exceeding $2,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 2 years for a subsequent offence. s 8LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

#### 11 Publication of false statements

(1) A person shall not make, repeat or publish a false statement where such:

(a) statement is of a nature likely to agitate, distress or create resentment or dissension among the community; and

(b) person knew or reasonably might have known that such statement or rumour was of a nature that would agitate, distress or create resentment or dissension among the community.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $3,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $3,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years for a subsequent offence.

#### 12 Insulting remarks as to genealogy or ancestry

(1) A person shall not make any remark, publish or cause to be published any insulting or derogatory words concerning the genealogy, parentage or ancestry of a person, with the intent to:

(a) provoke a breach of peace; or

(b) insult, taunt, annoy or offend such person.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $3,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $3,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years for a subsequent offence. s 12 PUBLIC ORDER ACT 2025

## PART 3 — ENFORCEMENT

#### 13 Functions and powers of a police officer

A police officer may perform his or her functions, or exercise his or her powers under the Nauru Police Force Act 2025 for the purposes of enforcing this Act.

#### 14 Noise Abatement Direction

A Notice Abatement Direction issued under Section 9, shall inform the occupier of a private premises:

(a) the prescribed terms to be complied with;

(b) the manner in which the Direction may be enforced; and

(c) that the failure to comply with the terms of the Direction shall be prima facie evidence of an offence against Section 9.

## PART 4 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 15 Exemption to noise nuisance

(1) Notwithstanding Sections 8 and 9, an exempt activity prescribed by regulations shall not constitute a noise nuisance for the purposes of this Part.

(2) A person who carries out an exempt activity shall not be liable for an offence under Sections 8 and 9.

(3) A person shall not undertake an exempt activity where the person does not have an exempt activity licence.

(4) The Commissioner of Police with the approval of the Minister, may issue an exempt activity licence to a person for an exempt activity and such licence shall state the duration for which the exempt activity may be undertaken.

(5) A person who contravenes subsection (3) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine of:

(a) $1,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months, or both for a first offence; and

(b) more than $1,000 but not exceeding $5,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 3 years, for a subsequent offence.

[s 15 subst Act 14 of 2026 s 6, opn 31 Mar 2026]

#### 16 Regulations

(1) The Cabinet may make regulations prescribing all matters necessary or convenient to be prescribed to give effect to this Act.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Cabinet may make regulations to provide for matters relating to:

(a) noise abatement direction, including the form to be used for such directions; and

(b) fixed penalties including:

(i) offences for which fixed penalties shall be imposed;

(ii) penalties for each corresponding prescribed fixed penalty offences;

(iii) fixed penalty notice which may be served to an offender for an offence for which a fixed penalty is prescribed;

(iv) a time frame for the payment of prescribed fixed penalty;

(v) procedure for the prosecution of and additional penalties to be imposed by the court on offenders defaulting to pay the prescribed penalty in accordance with the fixed penalty notice;

(vi) procedures for dealing with fixed penalty notice;

(vii) powers of police officers to enforce such fixed penalty notice; and

(viii) any other matters in relation to the effective enforcement of the fixed penalty notice.

## PART 3 — PROCEDURE FOR APPLYING, CONSIDERING, APPROVING, REFUSING AND CANCELLING AN EXEMPT ACTIVITY LICENCE

#### 5 Application for an exempt activity licence

(1) A person may apply to the Commissioner for an exempt activity licence in Form 1 of Schedule 2 providing the following information:

(a) full name;

(b) details of the applicant’s citizenship;

(c) residential address;

(d) email address if available;

(e) contact phone number;

(f) location of the activity;

(g) name and purpose of the activity;

(h) date and timeframe of the activity;

(i) the details of the activity, including any measures to minimise the impacts of the activity on the public and close vicinity residents;

(j) consent of at least 5 surrounding residents;

(k) a declaration of the individual’s intention to comply with all the written laws of the Republic;

(l) the fee in Schedule 4; and

(m) any other information the Commissioner may require.

(2) A person shall not provide false or misleading information in the application.

(3) A person who contravenes subsection (2), commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to a fine not exceeding $1,000 or to an imprisonment term not exceeding 18 months or both.

#### 6 Procedure for approving or refusing an application

(1) Where the Commissioner receives an application under Regulation 5, the Commissioner shall consider the application and make a recommendation to the Minister on whether to approve or reject an application.

(2) Where the Minister receives an application under subregulation (1), the Minister shall review the application and recommendation of the Commissioner and:

(a) approve the application; or

(b) reject the application.

(3) The Commissioner shall issue an exempt activity licence in Form 2 of Schedule 2 where the Minister has approved an application.

(4) Where the Minister has rejected an application, the Commissioner shall inform the applicant in writing of the decision in Form 3 of Schedule 2.

(5) In determining an application under this Regulation, the Minister and Commissioner shall consider the requirements of the Act and these Regulations.

#### 7 Duration of the exempt activity licence

The duration of the exempt activity licence shall be specified in the licence.

#### 8 Cancellation of a licence

(1) The Commissioner may recommend to the Minister to cancel an exempt activity licence, where the Commissioner is satisfied that:

(a) the holder of a licence has been convicted of or issued with a fixed penalty notice for an offence under the Act and these Regulations;

(b) it is in the public interest;

(c) the holder of a licence is not complying with conditions of his or her licence; or

(d) the holder of a licence requests the Commissioner to cancel such licence.

(2) Where the Minister approves the cancellation of a licence under subregulation (1), the Commissioner shall cancel an exempt activity licence in Form 4 of Schedule 2.

#### 9 Licence conditions

(1) A holder of a licence shall at all times comply with the following conditions:

(a) operate only during specified times provided in the licence; and

(b) any such conditions as determined by the Commissioner.

(2) Where a holder of a licence fails to comply with any of the conditions under subregulation (1), the Commissioner may cancel the licence in accordance with the Act and these Regulations, in addition to any fixed penalty that may be imposed.

(3) A person who contravenes a condition under subregulation (1) commits an offence under Section 8 or 9 of the Act.

#### 10 Record keeping requirements

(1) The Commissioner shall keep a record of all matters required for documentation under these Regulations.

(2) The records shall be kept and maintained in:

(a) electronic or digital form capable of being produced in printed form; and

(b) physical form.

(3) The Commissioner shall keep and maintain the records for a period of at least 7 years.

(4) The Commissioner shall not disclose or share information with any third party except where it is required for a law enforcement agency, or any other official purpose, by the Republic or for any proceedings.

(5) Any information provided to the Republic under this Act, shall not be disclosed to any other person without the prior consent or approval of the Commissioner, except where the information is required as exhibits or evidence in court. reg 8LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

## PART 6 — FIXED PENALTY OFFENCES AND NOTICES

#### 13 Fixed Penalty Notice

(1) A fixed penalty notice shall comply with the following requirements:

(a) state the place, date and time of alleged offence;

(b) state the name and address of the person to whom the notice is issued;

(c) notify the person to whom the notice is issued of when and where the fixed penalty may be paid;

(d) require the person to whom the notice is issued to pay the amount due within 21 days and in the event of failure to pay the fixed penalty, legal proceedings shall be instituted within the next 14 days and the person may have legal representation to represent them or enter a written guilty plea;

(e) notify the person to whom the notice is issued that, in case of default in payment within the time specified in the notice, the court may, if the person is found guilty by the court, impose a penalty which is more than the fixed penalty for the offence; and

(f) shall bear the date on which it was served on the person charged as the case requires.

(2) A police officer may issue a fixed penalty notice in the Form under Schedule 5, to a person who commits an offence under these Regulations.

#### 14 Requirements for a fixed penalty notice

(1) A fixed penalty notice shall not be served more than 14 days after the day on which the offence is alleged to have been committed.

(2) The police officer who issues a fixed penalty notice shall cause a signed copy of the notice to be placed before the Nauru Revenue Office and District Court as specified in the notice not later than 7 days after the notice is issued.

(3) Where a fixed penalty notice served upon a person has not been accepted by payment of the fixed penalty within 21 days of the date of the fixed penalty notice, the notice shall be regarded for all purposes as a summons issued under the Criminal Procedure Act 1972.

(4) A person who is issued a fixed penalty notice by a police officer shall accept its service by signing for the service of the fixed penalty notice.

(5) A person who fails to comply with subsection (4), commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000.

(6) The issuance of a fixed penalty notice shall not be taken to prevent the institution of proceedings under any other provision of the Act and these Regulations.

(7) Where a fixed penalty notice served upon a person is not complied with and is placed before the court, the court may impose a penalty higher than the fixed penalty stipulated for the offence and such penalty shall:

(a) be commensurate with the seriousness of the offence; and

(b) deprive the offender of any benefits arising from the commission of the offence.

#### 15 Fixed penalty offences

An offence against any of the provisions under the Act and these Regulations shall be deemed to be a fixed penalty offence.

#### 16 Penalties for fixed penalty offences

For the purposes of Regulation 15, the fixed penalty for any offence under the Act or these Regulations is $100.

#### 17 Failure to pay fixed penalty

A person, who has been issued with a fixed penalty notice and fails to pay the fixed penalty as required by the Act and these Regulations shall be summoned to attend court.

#### 18 Fixed penalty notice court appearances

(1) Where a person who is summoned to attend court, pleads guilty on the date of the hearing to the fixed penalty offence in which he or she was issued a fixed penalty notice, the court shall:

(a) impose a penalty for the offence as provided under the Act;

(b) order court costs of $100; and

(c) any such other orders the court deems fit to ensure compliance of the order of the court not limited to committal.

(2) Where the person is not present and is unrepresented in court, a plea of guilty shall be recorded and the matter dealt with in accordance with the procedure of the court and the court shall:

(a) impose a penalty for the offence as provided under the Act;

(b) order court costs of $100; and

(c) any such other orders the court deems fit to ensure compliance of the order of the court not limited to committal.

(3) Where the person is present or represented by a legal practitioner and pleads not guilty, the matter shall be dealt with in accordance with the procedure of the court.

(4) Where the person is found guilty after the hearing of a fixed penalty offence under subregulation (3), the court shall:

(a) impose the penalty for that offence as provided under the Act;

(b) order court costs of $200; and

(c) any such other orders the court deems fit to ensure compliance of the order of the court not limited to committal.

#### 19 Evidence of acceptance of a Fixed Penalty Notice

In any proceedings, a certificate signed by the Registrar of Courts, Nauru Revenue or a police officer that the fixed penalty is or is not paid shall, unless the contrary is proved, be conclusive evidence of the matters stated in the certificate. reg 15LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

#### 20 Application of the Criminal Procedure Act 1972

The Criminal Procedure Act 1972 applies mutatis mutandis to any matter or proceedings under the Act and these Regulations. reg 20 PUBLIC ORDER REGULATIONS 2026
