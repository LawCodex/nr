# Referendum Procedures Act 2025

## Table of Amendments

The Referendum Procedures Act 2025 No 26 was certified and commenced on 25 July 2025.

Amending Legislation Certified Date of Commencement

An Act to provide for the conduct of a referendum.

Enacted by the Parliament of Nauru as follows:

## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Referendum Procedures Act 2025.

#### 2 Commencement

This Act commences on the date it is certified by the Speaker.

#### 3 Interpretation

‘Article 84 referendum’ means a referendum held on a proposed law in accordance with Article 84 of the Constitution;

‘Court’ means the Court of Disputed Returns established under the Electoral Act 2016;

‘Electoral Act’ means the Electoral Act 2016;

‘polling hours’ means the hours a polling place is open for voting at a referendum;

‘polling place’ means a place established as a polling place under Section 9 or place established for voting at a general election;

‘presiding officer’ means a person appointed to be a presiding officer under the Electoral Act;

‘proposed law’ means a proposed law to alter, or that will have the effect of altering, the Fifth Schedule of the Constitution or any of the provisions of the Constitution contained in the Fifth Schedule;

‘referendum’ means an Article 84 referendum;

‘referendum day’ means the date of a referendum fixed under Section 6;

‘returning officer’ has the same meaning given to it under the Electoral Act;

‘roll’ means a roll kept under Section 37 of the Electoral Act; and

‘voter’ has the same meaning given to it under the Electoral Act.

#### 4 Application of this Act

Unless the contrary intention appears, this Act applies to an Article 84 referendum.

#### 5 Electoral Commission to conduct the referendum

The Electoral Commission shall be responsible for undertaking an Article 84 referendum under this Act.

## PART 2 — PREPARING FOR A REFERENDUM

#### 6 Date of Article 84 referendum

(1) This Section applies only to an Article 84 referendum.

(2) Within 60 days after Parliament passes a proposed law, the Cabinet shall, by Gazette notice, fix the date for a referendum on the proposed law.

(3) The date fixed shall be:

(a) at least 60 days after the proposed law is passed; and

(b) within 12 months after the proposed law is passed.

(4) An Article 84 referendum for a proposed law may be undertaken concurrently with the General Elections held under Article 39 of the Constitution.

(5) Where a referendum is undertaken within the time provided in subsection

(4), the requirements under Article 84(5) of the Constitution, may be duly completed by a Speaker elected after a General Election.

#### 7 Additional information to be published in the Gazette

(1) The Cabinet shall in publishing the date for a referendum under Section 6, also publish the following information for an Article 84 referendum the:

(a) places;

(b) dates; and

(c) times, during which, the proposed law on which the referendum will be held is available for inspection by the public.

(2) The notice shall also include information that a person will not be entitled to vote at the referendum unless the person’s name appears on the roll.

(3) For the purposes of subsection (1), the person appointed by the Cabinet to make available the proposed law for inspection, shall upon request of a person provide a copy of the proposed law and any other related documents.

#### 8 Returning officer

(1) The Electoral Commission shall be the returning officer for the purposes of the referendum.

(2) The returning officer has the functions and powers conferred on the returning officer by this Act or the Electoral Act.

#### 9 Polling places

(1) At least 7 days before a referendum, the returning officer shall, by Gazette notice:

(a) establish each polling place for the referendum; and

(b) specify the polling hours for each polling place.

(2) In establishing and allocating polling places, the returning officer shall ensure:

(a) the polling place allocated for a roll is reasonably accessible to each voter whose name appears on that roll;

(b) sufficient polling places are established to ensure voting can be completed during the polling hours; and

(c) polling places are distributed in a way that ensures a voter can vote within a reasonable time after arriving at the polling place.

(3) A voter may only vote at the referendum at the polling place allocated for the roll on which the voter’s name appears.

#### 10 Presiding officers

(1) The presiding officers for the purposes of an Article 84 referendum shall be appointed in accordance with the requirements of the Electoral Act.

(2) A presiding officer appointed for the purposes of a General Election may also be appointed for the purposes of subsection (1).

#### 11 Persons authorised to be present at scrutiny and count

The scrutineers for the purposes of counting of the votes shall be those under Section 83 of the Electoral Act. s 9LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

## PART 3 — VOTING

#### 12 Application of the Electoral Act

(1) The Electoral Act 2016 applies with the necessary and appropriate modifications to the conduct of the referendum.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), the following modifications apply:

(a) a reference to an election of members is taken to be a reference to the referendum;

(b) a reference to a polling day is taken to be a reference to the referendum day;

(c) a reference to the returning officer is taken to be a reference to the returning officer under this Act;

(d) a reference to a presiding officer is taken to be a reference to a presiding officer under this Act;

(e) a reference to a ballot-paper, ballot-box or other thing is taken to be a reference to a ballot paper, ballot box or other corresponding thing under this Act; and

(f) a reference to Section 83 of the Electoral Act is taken to be a reference to Section 11 of this Act.

(3) Where there is a method prescribed under the Electoral Act for a person to vote in an election under that Act, if the person is unable to attend a polling place during polling hours, that method applies, with the necessary and appropriate modifications, to voting in a referendum.

#### 13 Form of ballot paper

The ballot paper for an Article 84 referendum shall be in Form 1 of the Schedule.

#### 14 How voting is conducted

(1) Voting at a referendum shall be conducted by ballot.

(2) A person who intends to:

(a) vote in favour of the proposed law shall place a ‘X’ next to ‘YES’; or

(b) vote against the proposed law, shall place a ‘X’ next to ‘NO’.

## PART 4 — RETURNS AND SCRUTINY

#### 15 Opening of ballot boxes

(1) Immediately after the close of polls, the presiding officer for a polling place shall take each ballot box at the polling place to a place specified by the returning officer.

(2) Each ballot box shall be opened by the returning officer in accordance with the requirements of the Electoral Act.

#### 16 Counting of votes

(1) The votes shall be counted by the persons appointed by the returning officer in the presence of the persons eligible to present under Section 83 of the Electoral Act.

(2) The returning officer may limit the number of persons who may be present in order to ensure the count, and scrutiny of the count, is not interfered with.

#### 17 Informal ballot paper

(1) A ballot paper is informal if:

(a) the ballot paper is not authenticated by the initials of the presiding officer in accordance with Section 82(1)(a) of the Electoral Act, as applied by this Section;

(b) the ballot paper has no vote indicated on it;

(c) it is not possible to determine the voter’s intended vote; or

(d) the ballot paper is marked in a way that identifies the voter.

(2) A ballot paper is not informal only because the returning officer has marked it in some way.

(3) An informal ballot paper is not a validly cast vote, and the returning officer shall disregard all informal ballot papers in counting the votes.

#### 18 Result of referendum

Within 3 days after all validly cast votes are counted, the returning officer shall:

(a) complete a certificate of the results in Form 2 of the Schedule;

(b) give the certificate to the Speaker; and

(c) publish the certificate in the Gazette.

#### 19 Certification of the proposed law following Article 84

referendum

(1) This Section applies if a proposed law is approved by not less than two-thirds of votes validly cast at an Article 84 referendum.

(2) The Speaker shall not certify the proposed law under Article 47 of the Constitution unless:

(a) the time for filing notice of a dispute under Section 21 has expired;

(b) any dispute filed has been finally determined by the Court; and

(c) the decision of the Court on any dispute does not alter the result of the referendum so that the proposed law is no longer approved by not less than two-thirds of votes. s 19LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAURU

## PART 5 — DISPUTES

#### 20 Court of Disputed Returns

(1) The Court of Disputed Returns has jurisdiction to hear and determine a dispute under this Part.

(2) The procedural rules for the Court of Disputed Returns mutatis mutandis apply also to a return for the referendum.

#### 21 Filing notice of dispute

(1) A person may dispute the validity of the result of a referendum by filing notice of the dispute with the Court within 5 days after the certificate of the result is published in the Gazette.

(2) The notice filed shall state the nature and basis of the dispute and be accompanied by a filing fee of:

(a) $100; or

(b) if another amount is prescribed, the other amount.

#### 22 Determination of dispute

(1) The Court shall hear and determine the dispute in accordance with the Electoral Act.

(2) In determining the dispute, the Court may do any of the following:

(a) dismiss whole or part of the dispute;

(b) set aside the result of the referendum and direct that a new referendum be held on the same question;

(c) allow or disallow ballot papers; or

(d) make any other order the Court considers just, including as to costs.

## PART 6 — OFFENCES

#### 23 Voting is compulsory

(1) A voter shall vote at a referendum.

(2) A voter who does not vote and is not covered by the exceptions in subsection (3), commits an offence and is liable to a penalty not exceeding $100.

(3) A voter shall vote at each referendum unless he or she:

(a) is absent from the Republic on polling day;

(b) is unable to vote because he or she is seriously ill or infirm;

(c) has another valid and sufficient reason for not voting;

(d) has not been able to secure voting through a proxy or any other alternative method under the Electoral Act; or

(e) there is no method prescribed under the Electoral Act for a person to vote in an election under that Act if the person is unable to attend a polling place during polling hours.

(4) Proceedings for an offence against subsection (2) shall not be commenced unless notice is given under Section 25 and the time for complying with the notice expires.

#### 24 Evidence of failure to vote

(1) The returning officer shall, as soon as possible after referendum day, prepare and sign a list of voters who did not vote at the referendum.

(2) The signed list is evidence of the fact that a voter whose name appears on the list did not vote.

#### 25 Notice of failure to vote

(1) The returning officer, shall give each voter whose name appears on the list mentioned in Section 24 a notice in the Form 3 of the Schedule.

(2) The voter shall comply with the notice.

(3) A voter who fails to comply with the requirements of subsection (2) commits an offence and shall be liable to a fine of $100.

(4) The voter shall not give false or misleading information in purported compliance with the notice.

(5) A voter who provides false or misleading information in purported compliance with the notice for the purposes of subsection (4), commits an offence and shall be liable to a fine of $500.

#### 26 Application of Criminal Procedure Act 1972

The Criminal Procedure Act 1972 applies mutatis mutandis to any matter or proceedings under this Act.

## PART 7 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 27 Regulations

(1) The Cabinet may make regulations under this Act that are necessary or convenient to be prescribed to give effect to this Act.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), regulations may be prescribed to amend the forms in the Schedule.

#### 28 Repeal

The Referendum Procedures Act 2009 is repealed.

#### 29 Transitional Provisions

This Act applies to a proposed law passed by Parliament before or after the commencement of this Act.
