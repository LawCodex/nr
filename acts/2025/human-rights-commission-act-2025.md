## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Human Rights Commission Act 2025.

#### 2 Commencement

This Act commences on certification by the Speaker and after it has been brought into effect by the Cabinet in a notice published in the Gazette.

#### 3 Interpretation

In this Act:

‘Chief Human Rights Commissioner’ means the Chief Human Rights Commissioner appointed under Section 9(8);

‘Commission’ means the Human Rights Commission established under Section 7;

‘Commissioners’ means the Human Rights Commissioners appointed under Sections 9(8) and 10, and unless excluded includes the Chief Human Rights Commissioner;

‘complaint’ means a complaint made under Section 27;

‘complainant’ means a person who made a complaint or the person making a complaint on behalf of any other person under Section 26;

‘conduct’ includes any act or practice or any omission or failure to act in relation to a person’s legal functions and powers;

‘Constitution’ means the Constitution of the Republic of Naoero;

[def am Act 21 of 2026 s 5, opn 12 May 2026]

‘detention’ includes imprisonment, home detention and any other means by which a person’s freedom of movement is constrained;

‘human rights’ includes:

(a) the fundamental rights and freedoms contained in Part II of the Constitution; and

(b) customary international human rights law and the rights contained in international human rights treaties, conventions and other similar instruments, to which the Republic is a party and has given effect to it under domestic laws;

‘legal practitioner’ has the same meaning as it has in the Legal Practitioners Act 2019;

‘meetings of the Commission’ includes any meeting of the Commission for administrative, investigative or mediation or conciliation purposes;

‘Minister’ means the Minister for Justice and Border Control;

‘respondent’ includes a natural or legal person or a public body or a body exercising public functions, which is alleged to have violated the human rights of a person;

‘serious offence’ means an offence which has a term of imprisonment of 12 months or more; and

‘violation’ means violation or breach of human rights.

#### 4 Guiding principles of interpretation and application of this Act

The spirit and intent of Article 3 of the Constitution and the Republic’s motto ‘God’s Will First’ shall be the guiding principles in the interpretation and application of this Act.

#### 5 Objectives

The objectives of this Act are to:

(a) promote respect for human rights;

(b) promote the protection, development and attainment of human rights;

(c) recognise that human rights are an essential element of democracy and an inclusive society;

(d) establish the Human Rights Commission;

(e) promote the enforcement of human rights enshrined in the Constitution;

(f) provide for the jurisdiction of the Human Rights Commission;

(g) implement, promote and enforce human rights by domesticating the obligations under various international human rights treaties, conventions and other relevant instruments to which the Republic is a party; and

(h) recognise that human rights are not absolute but are limited by the provisions of the Constitution and the Republic’s motto ‘God’s Will First’, respect for the rights and dignity of other persons and which is justified in a free and a democratic society based on human dignity, equality, freedom and protection of the law.

## PART 2 — HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION

#### 7 Establishment of the Human Rights Commission

(1) The Human Rights Commission is established.

(2) The Commission has perpetual succession and may:

(a) sue and be sued;

(b) enter into contracts and other legal obligations;

(c) acquire, hold, manage and dispose of real or personal properties; and

(d) exercise such other powers of a corporation as set out in Schedule 1 to the Corporations Act 1972.

#### 8 Independence and impartiality

(1) The Commission is an independent and impartial institution.

(2) The Commissioners and the members of the staff of the Commission shall:

(a) subject to the Constitution and any other written law, perform the functions and exercise the powers of the Commission impartially and independently;

(b) not use the position or privilege of the office of the Commission for private gain or to benefit any other person improperly; and

(c) not conduct themselves in any manner that may compromise the independence, impartiality, transparency and integrity of the Commission.

(3) Where a Commissioner or a member of the staff of the Commission has a pecuniary or other interest in a matter before the Commission which may preclude him or her from performing any functions or exercising any powers fairly or impartially, he or she shall disclose the interest and shall not conduct an investigation, render assistance or participate in any decision-making in relation to the matter.

(4) Where a Commissioner or a member of the staff of the Commission fails to disclose an interest and performs or exercises his or her functions and powers under this Act, the Commission shall rectify the same as it deems appropriate to ensure a fair and impartial investigation or decision is made.

(5) For the purposes of transparency and accountability, a Commissioner shall register annually with the Commission a list of his or her pecuniary or other interest.

(6) The Director of Human Rights shall maintain a register of:

(a) disclosures of conflicts of interest by the Commissioners and members of the staff of the Commission under subsection (3); and

(b) pecuniary or other interests of the Commissioners and members of the staff of the Commission under subsection (5).

(7) The register shall be accessible to the public in the manner determined by the Commission.

#### 9 Composition of the Commission

(1) The Commission consists of the following Commissioners:

(a) the Chief Human Rights Commissioner; and

(b) 2 other Commissioners.

(2) The Chief Human Rights Commissioner shall be:

(a) a person qualified to be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court of Naoero; or

(b) a person with knowledge and experience in the protection of human rights.

[subs (2) am Act 21 of 2026 s 5, opn 12 May 2026]

(3) The 2 other Commissioners shall be persons:

(a) who have knowledge and understanding of Naoero culture, tradition and language; and

(b) who are committed to promoting human rights.

[subs (3) am Act 21 of 2026 s 5, opn 12 May 2026]

(4) The Commissioners shall be appointed through a competitive and transparent recruitment process to enable the President to make a nomination under subsection (5).

(5) The President shall refer the names of the nominees recommended to him or her following the recruitment process under subsection (4), to the Speaker, who shall within 14 days appoint a committee for the purposes of considering the nominations by the President.

(6) Where the committee fails or refuses to confirm the nominations under subsection (5), the Speaker shall refer the matter to the next sitting of the Parliament.

(7) Where the matter is referred to the next sitting of the Parliament under subsection (6), a member of the Parliament may nominate persons in accordance with subsections (2) and (3) and the Parliament shall decide by a simple majority whether to confirm the nominations.

(8) Where nominations under subsection (5) or (7) are confirmed, the President shall appoint the Commissioners.

(9) For avoidance of any doubt, a member of the Parliament may nominate a person whose name was referred by the President under subsection (5).

#### 10 Appointment of part time Commissioners

Subject to Section 9, the President may nominate part time Commissioners when necessary to ensure the Commission is able to perform its functions and exercise its powers under this Act.

#### 11 Disqualification of person to be appointed as a Commissioner

Subject to Section 9, a person is not eligible to be appointed as a Commissioner if the person:

(a) holds any public office in the Republic;

(b) is an undischarged bankrupt;

(c) has a physical or mental impairment or incapacity which prevents such person from performing the functions and exercising the powers of the Commission;

(d) has a criminal conviction for a serious offence;

(e) had been removed as a Commissioner on his or her earlier appointment to the Commission; or

(f) is a candidate in an election for the Parliament or is duly elected and serving as a member of the Parliament. s 9LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

#### 12 Commissioner not to act in certain circumstances

(1) Where a Commissioner is charged for one or more serious offences under the Crimes Act 2016 or any other written law, he or she shall not perform any functions or exercise any powers under this Act.

(2) Where the Commissioner is unable to perform any functions or exercise any powers under subsection (1), he or she shall not be paid any remuneration or benefits.

(3) Where any charge under subsection (1) has not been heard and determined within 6 months from the date on which the criminal proceeding is commenced in the court, the remaining period of the Commissioner’s term of office is deemed to have lapsed, provided the delay in prosecution beyond the 6 months is not due to any fault or failure on the part of the Commissioner.

(4) Where the Commissioner is acquitted or cleared of all charges within 6 months, he or she shall resume office with immediate effect together with the payment of any remuneration or benefits not paid under subsection (2).

#### 13 Term of office

(1) The Commissioners shall:

(a) hold office for a period of 4 years; and

(b) be eligible for re-appointment for an additional term.

(2) A Commissioner may resign from office by giving 3 months’ notice of resignation in writing to the Minister unless such period is abridged by the Minister.

(3) The office of one or more of the Commissioners shall not remain vacant for any period exceeding 3 months from the date of the resignation, removal or expiry of the term of such appointment.

#### 14 Acting appointment

(1) The Chief Human Rights Commissioner shall appoint one of the Commissioners to act as the Chief Human Rights Commissioner in his or her absence until his or her return to office.

(2) Subject to subsection (1), where the office of the Chief Human Rights Commissioner becomes vacant or he or she is unable to perform the duties of the office, then until a person is appointed and has resumed the functions and powers of the office, such functions and powers shall be performed and exercised by the most senior of the remaining Commissioners based on their respective dates of appointment.

#### 15 Removal

A Commissioner may not be removed from office except on a resolution of Parliament approved by not less than two-thirds of the total number of members of Parliament seeking his or her removal from office on any of the following grounds:

(a) misconduct or breach of code of conduct;

(b) physical or mental impairment or incapacity preventing the Commissioner from performing the functions and exercising the powers of the Commission; s 15 HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION ACT 2025

(c) criminal conviction; or

(d) wilful and intentional failure to declare a conflict of interest.

#### 16 Remuneration

(1) The remuneration and benefits of the Commissioners and members of the staff of the Commission shall be a direct charge on the Treasury Fund.

(2) The remuneration and benefits of the Commissioners shall be determined by the Cabinet and prescribed by regulations.

(3) The remuneration, benefits and any other terms and conditions of the office of the Commissioners and the members of the staff of the Commission shall not be reduced or adversely affected during the term of such office.

#### 17 Vacancies

(1) The office of a Commissioner becomes vacant:

(a) on the expiry of the term of the office of the Commissioner;

(b) on the death of the Commissioner;

(c) on the removal from office of the Commissioner;

(d) on the resignation of the Commissioner;

(e) where the Commissioner is adjudged bankrupt;

(f) where the Commissioner is nominated to be a candidate in an election for the Parliament or elected as a member of the Parliament; or

(g) where the Commissioner has been convicted by a court in the Republic or elsewhere of a serious offence or any provisions of this Act.

(2) Subject to Section 9, a vacancy in the office of the Commission shall not affect the validity of the proceedings or decisions of the Commission.

(3) Where a vacancy occurs under subsection (1), the Chief Human Rights Commissioner shall, as soon as practicable, inform the Minister in writing of such vacancy so that it can be filled in accordance with Section 9 or 10.

#### 18 Oath or affirmation of office

A Commissioner, upon being appointed, shall subscribe to the oath or affirmation of office in the Schedule.

#### 19 Funds of the Commission

(1) The funds for the operations of the Commission shall be:

(a) as appropriated by the Parliament; or

(b) any such other funds including grants or aid.

(2) The Director of Human Rights shall be the accounting officer of the Commission and is charged with the responsibility of financial accountability under the Public Finance (Control and Management) Act 1997 and the Treasury Fund Protection Act 2004. s 15LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 3 — FUNCTIONS AND POWERS OF THE COMMISSION

#### 20 Functions of the Commission

The functions of the Commission are to:

(a) develop, conduct or manage information and education programmes to foster public understanding and awareness of Part II of the Constitution, this Act and the role and activities of the Commission;

(b) coordinate with institutions, bodies or authorities with similar objectives to the Commission in order to foster common policies and practices in the handling of complaints in cases of overlapping jurisdictions;

(c) liaise and interact with instrumentalities and other bodies of the Republic which actively promote respect for human rights including civil societies to further the objectives of this Act;

(d) engage with regional and international human rights bodies, organisations or institutions;

(e) where practical, review and make recommendations on government policies relating to human rights;

(f) monitor the implementation of and compliance with regional and international treaties, conventions and other instruments relating to the objectives of this Act;

(g) prepare and submit annual and other reports to the Parliament on the state of human rights in the Republic;

(h) consider recommendations and requests from third parties or other persons concerning the promotion of respect for human rights;

(i) carry out such studies, surveys or consultations concerning human rights as may be referred to it by the Parliament, the Cabinet, the President or the Minister and submit duly completed reports with recommendations as it deems appropriate;

(j) review and make recommendations to the Minister on human rights related legislation;

(k) promote homogeneity of the application of human rights to all persons including women, children and persons with disabilities;

(l) receive complaints of alleged violations and where necessary report such violations to the relevant authorities including the Parliament and the Cabinet;

(m) put in place proposals by the Commission or any other relevant authorities to end any violation;

(n) investigate alleged violations on its own accord or on a complaint;

(o) appear or bring proceedings before a court under this Act;

(p) intervene in any judicial proceedings involving human rights issues to assist the court or a party in the proceedings, whether on its own accord with the leave of the court or on the invitation of the court;

(q) investigate and report to the court on any matters which the court requests the Commission to provide assistance with;

(r) mediate or conciliate any complaints relating to an alleged violation;

(s) receive and invite representations from members of the public on any matter affecting human rights;

(t) when required, visit or have access to and make reports on places of detention;

(u) advise the Minister on matters relevant to the operations of the Commission and administration of this Act; and

(v) perform such other functions conferred on the Commission under this Act or any other written law.

#### 21 Powers of the Commission

The Commission shall have the power to do all things necessary or convenient to be done for the performance of its functions under this Act or any other written law.

#### 22 Delegation of functions and powers

(1) Subject to subsection (5), the Commission may, in writing, delegate the performance of its functions or exercise of its powers to:

(a) a Commissioner;

(b) the Director of Human Rights;

(c) a member of the staff of the Commission; or

(d) an advisory body or person of special expertise.

(2) The delegation shall be subject to such conditions and directions imposed by the Commission but it shall not divest the Commission from performing those functions and exercising those powers.

(3) The delegation shall be given under the hand of the Chief Human Rights Commissioner.

(4) The Commission may confirm, vary or revoke any such decisions or conduct of the delegate.

(5) The Commission shall not delegate its power to delegate under this Section.

#### 23 Staff of the Commission

(1) The Chief Human Rights Commissioner, in consultation with the other 2 Commissioners, shall:

(a) appoint the Director of Human Rights, who is the administrative head of the Commission; and

(b) appoint such other members of the staff of the Commission as may be necessary to assist in performing the functions and exercising the powers of the Commission.

(2) Where the office of the Director of Human Rights is vacant or he or she is unable to perform the duties of the office, the Chief Human Rights Commissioner shall appoint a member of the staff of the Commission to perform the functions and exercise the powers of the Director of Human Rights.

(3) The Commission shall determine the minimum qualification requirements of the members of the staff of the Commission appointed under subsection (1).

(4) The Commission shall fix the remuneration and benefits of the members of the staff of the Commission and such remuneration and benefits shall, in so far as practicable, be commensurate with the public service salary scale.

(5) The Commission shall have the power to discipline or terminate the employment of any member of the staff of the Commission. s 20LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

(6) For the purposes of subsection (5), the Commission shall, within 3 months of initial appointment under Section 9, prescribe a code of conduct and disciplinary offences and proceedings for its staff.

(7) The code of conduct in subsection (6) shall apply to the Commissioners.

#### 24 Functions and powers of the Director of Human Rights

(1) The Director of Human Rights shall have the following functions:

(a) the management of the affairs and operations of the Commission;

(b) the formation and development of a corporate and strategic plan for the Commission;

(c) the organisation, management and administrative control over all other members of the staff of the Commission;

(d) implementing the directions and decisions of the Commission;

(e) compiling and maintaining the records of the Commission;

(f) to be the secretary to the Commission; and

(g) such other functions which the Commission may from time to time confer or delegate in order to achieve the objectives of this Act.

(2) The Director of Human Rights shall have the power to do all things necessary or convenient to be done for the performance of his or her functions under this Act or any other written law. s 24 HUMAN RIGHTS COMMISSION ACT 2025

## PART 4 — MEETINGS OF THE COMMISSION

#### 25 Meetings of the Commission

(1) The Commission shall determine the times and places for the meetings of the Commission.

(2) Where the Director of Human Rights is not able to perform the secretarial duties for the Commission, he or she shall appoint any other member of the staff of the Commission to act as and perform all such duties of the secretary.

(3) The quorum for a meeting of the Commission shall be 2 Commissioners.

(4) The decision of the majority of the Commissioners present at the meeting shall be the decision of the Commission and where there is a tie in the number of votes, the Chief Human Rights Commissioner has a casting vote in addition to his or her deliberative vote.

(5) The Commission shall:

(a) determine its own procedures for its meetings; and

(b) cause minutes of its meetings to be kept.

## PART 5 — COMPLAINTS

#### 26 Who may make complaint

(1) The following persons may complain to the Commission:

(a) an individual who is the subject of an alleged violation; or

(b) a guardian or representative of the individual who is the subject of an alleged violation.

(2) Subject to Section 61, the Commission may, on its own initiative, commence an investigation in respect of any alleged violation.

#### 27 How to make complaint

A complaint made or referred to the Commission shall:

(a) be in writing or a statement recorded by a member of the staff of the Commission and signed by the complainant;

(b) state the complainant’s name, residential address, email address and telephone number;

(c) provide detailed particulars of the alleged violation to which the complaint relates; and

(d) provide the name or identity of the alleged respondent.

#### 28 Commission may decline to investigate or continue to

investigate particular complaint The Commission may decline to investigate or continue with an investigation where it has already commenced where:

(a) the complaint is frivolous, vexatious or unmeritorious;

(b) the complaint is not within the jurisdiction of the Commission;

(c) the complainant or person acting on behalf of the complainant has commenced proceedings relating to the same complaint in a court or any other appropriate body established under a written law;

(d) the complainant informs the Commission in writing of his or her intention not to proceed with the complaint;

(e) a substantial delay has occurred since the alleged violation which may prejudice a fair investigation or consideration of the complaint;

(f) the complainant fails to comply with any request made by the Commission during the investigation; or

(g) the complainant cannot be contacted by the Commission.

#### 29 Notice about declining investigation of complaint

Where the Commission declines to investigate or continue with an investigation of a complaint, the Commission shall inform the complainant in writing of its decision and the reasons for such decision.

#### 30 Restrictions on reporting complaints dealt with by

Commission Where the Commission declines to investigate or to continue with an investigation and has informed the complainant of its decision, the Commission shall not receive further complaints on the same issue or facts by the same complainant or on behalf of the same complainant. s 30LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 6 — INVESTIGATION OF COMPLAINT

#### 31 Commission to investigate complaint

Subject to Section 28, the Commission shall investigate a complaint.

#### 32 Respondent to be notified of complaint

(1) The Commission shall inform a respondent in writing:

(a) of the complaint made against the respondent;

(b) of the nature and particulars of the complaint;

(c) to provide a response to the complaint;

(d) to provide documents, materials or such other information relevant to the complaint; and

(e) that it may take such other steps as may be necessary under this Act.

(2) A respondent shall comply with the requirements of the notice under subsection (1) unless the respondent has any lawful reason or justification not to do so, in which case the respondent shall inform the Commission of such reason or justification.

#### 33 Discovery or obtaining of information from third parties

(1) The Commission may, by notice in writing, require a third party which may have in its possession, power or control any documents, materials or any other information which may be relevant for the purposes of investigating a complaint, to provide to the Commission such documents, materials or information.

(2) The third party shall comply with a request made under subsection (1) unless the third party has any lawful reason or justification not to comply with such request.

#### 34 Commission to inform any person of self-incrimination or

liability Where during the course of an investigation it appears to the Commission that a person may incriminate himself or herself or become liable to a penalty or damages, the Commission shall afford such person an opportunity to be heard in relation to the complaint by providing evidence or making representations in person or by a legal representative.

## PART 7 — MEDIATION OR CONCILIATION OF COMPLAINT

#### 35 Commission may conduct mediation or conciliation

(1) The Commission shall do its best to resolve a complaint.

(2) For the purpose of subsection (1), the Commission may convene a mediation or conciliation of the parties.

(3) Where the Commission decides to convene a mediation or conciliation, it:

(a) shall issue a notice to the complainant and respondent respectively to attend such mediation or conciliation; and

(b) may require any other person who may assist or tender any evidence or materials to attend the mediation or conciliation.

(4) Where the complainant, respondent or a person is required to attend a mediation or conciliation, he or she shall attend such mediation or conciliation.

(5) Where:

(a) the complainant fails to attend the mediation or conciliation without informing the Commission or without any lawful reason or justification, the Commission may dismiss the complaint; and

(b) the respondent fails to attend the mediation or conciliation, the Commission may proceed to making its findings as part of the investigation and include it as part of the report.

#### 36 Representation

(1) A complainant or a respondent may be represented by any other person or legal practitioner for the purposes of a mediation or conciliation.

(2) The Commission may hold mediation or conciliation in the absence of any representative under subsection (1).

#### 37 Interpreters

Where the Commission deems necessary that an interpreter is required for the purposes of conducting a mediation or conciliation, the Commission shall make such an interpreter available during the course of the mediation or conciliation.

#### 38 Commission not bound by strict rules of evidence

In conducting a mediation or conciliation, the Commission shall not be bound by the strict rules of evidence.

#### 39 Mediation or conciliation may not be open to public

Where the Commission deems appropriate, it shall convene a mediation or conciliation in private or restrict the presence of any person.

#### 40 Mediation or conciliation agreement

(1) Where as a result of mediation or conciliation the parties agree to resolve a complaint:

(a) the Commission shall prepare the terms and conditions of such agreement;

(b) the complainant and respondent or their representatives shall execute the agreement; and

(c) such agreement shall be endorsed by the Chief Human Rights Commissioner.

(2) The parties or the Commission may, with the leave of the Supreme Court, register and enforce the agreement in the Supreme Court in the same manner as a judgment, decision or order of the Supreme Court is enforced.

#### 41 Complaints not resolved by mediation or conciliation

(1) Where a complaint is not resolved by mediation or conciliation, the Commission shall inform the parties that the complaint is not capable of being resolved by mediation or conciliation.

(2) Where a complaint is not resolved by way of mediation or conciliation:

(a) the Commission may conduct further investigation;

(b) the Commission or the complainant may seek redress through any other appropriate means; or

(c) the Commission may make appropriate recommendations in writing to the parties.

#### 42 Notice about resolved complaint

The Commission shall, as soon as practicable after the complaint is resolved, inform the complainant and respondent in writing stating the outcome of the resolution of the complaint including any agreement duly executed between the parties.

#### 43 Confidentiality of mediation or conciliation

Except for the agreement duly executed by the parties under Section 40, any thing said or done in the course of a mediation or conciliation of a complaint is not admissible in any criminal, civil or administrative proceeding, unless the complainant and respondent agree to it being admissible. s 40LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 8 — REPORTING REQUIREMENTS

#### 44 Annual report

(1) The Commission shall provide to the Minister an annual report on the performance and exercise of its functions and powers under this Act on or before 28 February of each year.

(2) The annual report shall include the following:

(a) the number of complaints made under Section 27;

(b) the nature of the complaints;

(c) the number of complaints not investigated or continued to be investigated under Section 28;

(d) the number of mediations or conciliations conducted by the Commission;

(e) the number of complaints resolved and unresolved by mediation or conciliation;

(f) the range of relief given to the complainants;

(g) the number of unresolved complaints by way of investigation;

(h) the number and nature of third-party discoveries or disclosure of information;

(i) details of any examination of the interaction between this Act, Constitution and any other written law;

(j) the nature and number of public education and awareness on human rights undertaken by the Commission; and

(k) the number of cases in which the Commission:

(i) intervened in court proceedings on its own accord or on the invitation of the Court;

(ii) assisted one or more parties in a court proceeding; and

(iii) commenced proceedings on its own accord.

(3) The annual report may include other information the Commission deems appropriate.

#### 45 Other reports

(1) The Commission may prepare a report about a matter relevant to the performance of its functions and exercise of its powers.

(2) The Commission shall provide a report prepared under subsection (1) to the Minister as soon as practicable.

(3) A report under this Section shall not include personal information about an individual unless the information has been published previously or provided for the purpose of publication by the individual.

#### 46 Report containing adverse comment

(1) Where the Commission proposes to make an adverse comment about a person in a report, the Commission shall give the person an opportunity to provide a written response to the Commission about the proposed adverse comment.

(2) Where the person provides a response and the Commission includes the adverse comment, the Commission shall ensure the person’s response is stated in the report.

(3) For the purpose of this Section, an adverse comment does not include a statement that a person did not participate in resolving a complaint.

#### 47 Minister to table reports

The Minister shall table the annual report and each report given to the Minister in the Parliament at the next sitting of the Parliament. s 46LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 9 — PROCEEDINGS RELATED TO ENFORCEMENT OF HUMAN RIGHTS

#### 48 Claims for breach of human rights

(1) Any person may, independent of this Act, commence a proceeding in the Supreme Court for the application, interpretation and enforcement of any violation of his or her human rights in accordance with the requirements prescribed by any written law and rules of the Supreme Court.

(2) Where an issue relating to any violation is alleged in a proceeding to be commenced or already filed, subject to the Constitution, such issue shall be heard and considered by the Supreme Court within that proceeding.

#### 49 Power of the Commission to commence proceedings

(1) The Commission may commence proceedings in the Supreme Court under Part 7 of the Supreme Court Act 2018 for the determination of the following:

(a) any question on the interpretation or application of law in relation to human rights; or

(b) interpretation and application of Part II of the Constitution in relation to human rights under Article 14 of the Constitution.

(2) The Commission may commence a proceeding on behalf of a complainant or represent a complainant in a proceeding.

(3) The Commission may engage the services of a legal practitioner for the purposes of this Part.

## PART 10 — OFFENCES

#### 50 Compliance with requirements of notice

(1) A person shall comply with the requirements of any notice issued by the Commission to provide information, documents or attend before the Commission at its meeting in relation to a complaint.

(2) Where a person fails to comply with subsection (1) without any lawful reason or justification, such person shall be liable to an administrative fine of $1,000 to be paid to the Commission within 7 days of the default of such compliance.

(3) A person who fails to comply with subsections (1) and (2) without any lawful reason or justification commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $5,000 or a term of imprisonment not exceeding 24 months or both.

#### 51 Interfering, hindering, threatening or obstructing the

Commission

(1) A person shall not interfere with, hinder, threaten or obstruct the Commission or a member of the staff of the Commission in the performance of the Commission’s functions or exercise of the Commission’s powers.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $25,000 or a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years or both.

#### 52 False information or misrepresentation

(1) A person shall not provide false or misleading information or misrepresent or knowingly mislead the Commission or a member of the staff of the Commission in the performance of the functions or exercise of the powers of the Commission.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $25,000 or a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years or both.

#### 53 Failure to attend or provide documents

(1) A person, after having been requested, shall not fail, without any lawful reason or justification, to appear or to produce documents, books or information in his or her custody or control, before the Commission.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $25,000 or a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years or both.

#### 54 Victimisation, intimidation or harassment

(1) A person shall not intentionally victimise, intimidate, threaten or harass any other person on the ground that such person or any associate of that person:

(a) intends to make use of his or her rights under this Act;

(b) has made use of his or her rights or promoted the rights of some other person under this Act;

(c) has given information or evidence on a complaint, investigation or proceeding under this Act;

(d) has declined to do an act which would breach this Act; or

(e) has done any thing under this Act.

## PART 11 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 55 Disclosure prohibited under certain circumstances

(1) Any document or information which is subject to privilege on the grounds of public interest immunity, national security and under the Offıcial Information Act 1976 may not be disclosed unless duly approved by the relevant authority having the power, custody or possession of the same or upon an order of the court.

(2) A certificate issued by the Minister certifying that any document or information under subsection (1) is prohibited from disclosure or discovery is conclusive evidence of such prohibition.

(3) The following documents fall in the class of documents or information which shall not be disclosed:

(a) matters relating to the security, defence or international relations of the Republic;

(b) any special or secret sources of investigation or detection of offences;

(c) Cabinet submissions, deliberations, decisions and other associated documents or information; or

(d) any other documents or information or class of documents or information declared by the Cabinet to be prohibited from disclosure.

#### 56 Confidentiality of information

(1) The Commission and the members of the staff of the Commission shall maintain confidence in respect of all matters that come to their knowledge in the performance of their functions and exercise of their powers.

(2) Subject to subsection (1), the Commission may disclose in any report made by the Commission under this Act, any matter that in the opinion of the Commission may be disclosed in order to establish grounds for the Commission’s findings and recommendations.

(3) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to a fine not exceeding $5,000 or a term of imprisonment not exceeding 24 months or both.

#### 57 Anonymity

(1) If at any time the Commission deems that the anonymity of a person involved in a complaint is necessary for the person’s work security, privacy or any human right, the Commission may give a direction prohibiting the disclosure of information that identifies or is likely to lead to the identification of such person.

(2) In this Section, ‘involved in complaint’ means:

(a) a complainant;

(b) a respondent;

(c) giving information to a person who is performing a function under this Act;

(d) a third party providing any information under this Act; and

(e) a relevant authority which has the power, custody or possession of relevant information.

#### 58 Complaints of violations against the Commission

(1) Where the Minister receives a complaint alleging any one or more conduct under Section 15 or an alleged violation by a Commissioner, the Minister shall refer the complaint to the Chief Justice for his or her investigation or consideration and provide the findings to the Minister.

(2) The Minister shall table the findings of the Chief Justice in the next sitting of the Parliament.

(3) Where a member of the staff of the Commission is alleged to have committed a violation, the Chief Human Rights Commissioner shall cause an investigation and appropriately deal with such complaint under the provisions of this Act.

#### 59 Protection from liability

The Commissioners, Director of Human Rights, members of the staff of the Commission or any other person authorised under this Act shall not be liable for any act done or omitted to be done in good faith in the performance of a function or exercise of a power under this Act or any other written law in furthering the objectives of this Act.

#### 60 Jurisdiction of the District Court for offences

The District Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine all offences under this Act and has the power to impose the penalties for the offences under this Act.

#### 61 Commission not to deal with certain matters

The Commission shall have no power, jurisdiction or authority to investigate, receive, advocate or defend:

(a) any matters relating to the definition of marriage under the Births, Deaths and Marriages Act Registration 2017;

(b) any matters relating to the identity of any person’s gender;

(c) a matter arising from or by a person being in the Republic under any written law due to the person being stateless, an asylum seeker or a refugee; or

(d) a matter arising from any arrangement or understanding the Republic has with another country in respect of any person.
