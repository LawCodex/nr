## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Community Monitoring Offıcers Act 2026.

#### 2 Commencement

This Act commences on the date it is certified by the Speaker.

#### 3 Objectives of this Act

The objectives of this Act are to:

(a) establish proportionate and rights-compatible community monitoring regime;

(b) authorise limited and clearly defined powers for Community Monitoring Officers;

(c) ensure accountability, oversight, and safeguards in the exercise of such powers; and

(d) to empower Community Monitoring Officers with such powers to maintain peace, law and order in the community consistent with Part 2 of the Constitution.

[s 3 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 5, opn 9 June 2026]

## PART 2 — COMMUNITY MONITORING OFFICERS

#### 5 Appointment of accredited service provider

(1) A service provider shall be approved by the Cabinet to provide services for the Government to achieve the objectives of this Act.

(2) The Minister may appoint such approved service provider for the purposes of providing services to the Government under this Act.

#### 6 Appointment of Community Monitoring Officers

(1) An approved service provider under Section 5 shall employ persons who have been approved by the Minister under this Section.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), the service provider shall seek approval from the Minister for engaging CMO.

(3) Where the Minister has approved the engagement of a CMO, the Commissioner shall publish in the gazette the appointment of such CMO.

[subs (3) am Act 22 of 2026 s 7, opn 9 June 2026]

(4) A service provider shall only seek approval under subsection (2), if the person has successfully completed a training approved by the Commissioner.

[subs (4) am Act 22 of 2026 s 7, opn 9 June 2026]

(5) An application under subsection (2) shall be accompanied by:

(a) an identification instrument of the CMO;

(b) a certificate certifying that the CMO has undertaken the necessary training;

(c) a police record showing the CMO has no criminal convictions; and

(d) such other requirements as may be prescribed.

(6) The service provider shall request and the Commissioner shall issue a CMO with an identity card under his or her hand.

[subs (6) am Act 22 of 2026 s 7, opn 9 June 2026]

(7) The Minister, may by notice published in the Gazette, revoke the appointment of a CMO where the CMO:

(a) has resigned or has been terminated from his or her employment by an approved service provider;

(b) fails to comply with the requirements of continuing training under subsection (5)(b);

(c) is convicted of a criminal offence; or

(d) no longer meets any prescribed requirements.

[subs (7) insrt Act 22 of 2026 s 7, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 7 Functions of a Community Monitoring Officer

The functions of a CMO are to:

(a) observe the movement and conduct of persons to ensure the safety of the community;

(b) report any unlawful conduct of a person to the police; and

(c) intervene in emergency situations to maintain peace, order and safety of all persons.

[s 7 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 8, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 8 Powers of Community Monitoring Officer

A Community Monitoring Officer shall have the power to do all things necessary or convenient to be done for the performance of his or her functions under this Act or any other written law.

## PART 3 — SPECIAL POWERS OF COMMUNITY MONITORING OFFICERS

#### 10 Obligation to provide name, address or other information to a

Community Monitoring Officer

(1) Where a CMO reasonably believes that a person has committed, is committing or intends to commit an offence, the CMO may ask and the person upon being asked by a CMO, shall provide his or her name, address or other information about his or her identity,

(2) A person upon being asked by a CMO, shall not provide a false name, address or false information.

(3) A person who contravenes subsections (1) and (2) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to an imprisonment term not exceeding 6 months.

[s 10 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 9, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 11 Directions by a Community Monitoring Officer

(1) Where a CMO reasonably believes that a person has committed, is committing or intends to commit an offence, the CMO for the purpose of ensuring community safety, may issue a direction to such person.

(2) A person who without lawful or reasonable excuse contravenes a direction issued under subsection (1), shall be reported by the CMO to the Police.

[s 11 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 10, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 12 Consent-based search

(1) Where a CMO reasonably believes that a person has committed, is committing or intends to commit an offence, the CMO may request a person’s consent to search the person or his or her belongings.

(2) The person shall be informed by a CMO that he or she may decline to give consent and provide a reasonable excuse for not providing such consent.

(3) Where a person does not give consent and the CMO reasonably believes that the person has committed, is committing or intends to commit an offence, the CMO shall immediately report such matter to the Police.

[s 12 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 11, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 13 Seizure of weapons and other offending materials

(1) Where during the course of a search under Section 12 a CMO discovers a weapon or offending material which is capable of being used for committing an offence, the CMO shall seize such weapon or offending material and report the matter to the Police.

(2) The CMO shall prepare an inventory for the seized weapon or other offending material, which shall be provided to the Nauru Police Force together with such weapon or offending material for the purposes of investigation, and prosecution.

(3) The service provider shall provide a report of any seizure done under this Section to the Commissioner, the details of which shall include:

(a) purpose and nature of the search;

(b) description of the weapon or offending material seized;

(c) the identity details of the person;

(d) the name of the CMO who conducted the search; and

(e) the matter being reported to the police including any weapons or offending material seized that has been handed over to the police for the purposes of investigation and prosecution.

[subs (2) am Act 22 of 2026 s 12, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 14 Use of reasonable force

(1) A CMO may use reasonable force to restrain a person in performing his or her functions or exercising his or her powers under this Act.

[subs (1) am Act 22 of 2026 s 12, opn 9 June 2026]

(2) The CMO shall report the incident to the Police.

(3) Upon receiving a report under subsection (2), the Police shall promptly assist the CMO where it is required to arrest the person.

[subs (3) am Act 22 of 2026 s 12, opn 9 June 2026]

(4) The CMO shall provide a report of an incident under this Section to the Commissioner, the details of which shall include:

(a) purpose and nature of the incident;

(b) the identity details of the person;

(c) the name of the CMO who was involved in the incident; and

(d) the matter being reported to the police.

## PART 4 — ROLE OF POLICE

#### 15 Police powers preserved

(1) Nothing in this Act, shall be construed as diminishing, restricting, or impairing the full powers, duties, and responsibilities vested in the Police under the Nauru Police Force Act 2025.

(2) The powers of CMOs granted under this Act, shall be supplementary and shall be exercised concurrently with, but not as a substitute for, the powers of the police under the Nauru Police Force Act 2025.

(3) Where a conflict arises as to the exercise of powers by CMOs and police officers, the authority of a police officer shall prevail.

#### 16 Standard operating procedures

The Commissioner may in cooperation with a department establish standard operating procedures for the purpose of enforcing this Act.

[s 16 subst Act 22 of 2026 s 14, opn 9 June 2026]

## PART 5 — SAFEGUARDS AND OVERSIGHT

#### 17 Oversight by the Commissioner

(1) The Commissioner shall be responsible for regulating the performance of functions and exercise of powers of CMOs.

[subs (1) am Act 22 of 2026 s 15, opn 9 June 2026]

(2) Where the Commissioner receives a complaint under Section 18, the Commissioner shall in accordance with the prescribed requirements, address such complaints.

[subs (2) am Act 22 of 2026 s 15, opn 9 June 2026]

[s 17 am Act 22 of 2026 s 15, opn 9 June 2026]

#### 18 Complaints may be made

(1) A person who is aggrieved with the performance of functions or exercise of powers of a CMO under this Act, may lodge a complaint with the Commissioner.

[subs (1) am Act 22 of 2026 s 15, opn 9 June 2026]

(2) Regulations shall be prescribed to provide for the procedures to address complaints that are made.

#### 19 Record keeping

(1) The Commissioner shall keep a record of all appointments made, reports received and complaint procedures undertaken.

[subs (1) am Act 22 of 2026 s 15, opn 9 June 2026]

(2) The records shall be kept and maintained in:

(a) electronic or digital form which is capable of being produced in printed form; or

(b) physical form.

(3) The Secretary shall not disclose or share any information with any person except where it is required by a law enforcement agency for official purposes, by the Republic, or any proceedings.

#### 20 Confidentiality of information supplied

(1) Any person or law enforcement agency who has requested and has been provided information under this Act shall ensure that such information obtained is kept confidential.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine not exceeding $30,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 2 years or to both.

#### 21 Commissioner shall report potential breach of visa conditions

Where the Commissioner has received a report from a CMO under this Act which is a potential breach of the a person’s visa conditions, the Commissioner shall report such matter to the Secretary for Justice and Border Control.

## PART 6 — OFFENCES

#### 22 Obstructing a Community Monitoring Officer

(1) A person shall not obstruct, hinder, intimidate or resist a CMO where the CMO is performing his or her functions or exercising his or her powers.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and shall be liable upon conviction to an imprisonment term not exceeding 2 years.

## PART 7 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 23 Regulations

(1) The Cabinet may make regulations prescribing all matters necessary or convenient to be prescribed to give effect to this Act.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Cabinet may make regulations to:

(a) offences for which fixed penalties shall be imposed;

(b) a fixed penalty for each corresponding prescribed fixed penalty offence;

(c) the fixed penalty notice which a CMO may serve to an offender for an offence for which a fixed penalty is prescribed;

(d) a time frame for the payment of a fixed penalty;

(e) procedure for the prosecution of and additional penalty to be imposed by the court on offenders defaulting to pay the prescribed penalty in accordance with the fixed penalty notice;

(f) procedures for dealing with a fixed penalty notice; and

(g) any other matters necessary for the effective enforcement of fixed penalty notices.
