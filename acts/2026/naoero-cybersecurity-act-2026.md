# Naoero Cybersecurity Act 2026

## Table of Amendments

The Naoero Cybersecurity Act 2026 No 38 was certified and commenced on 17 September 2026.

Amending Legislation Certified Date of Commencement

An Act to establish a legal framework to protect systems, data and critical infrastructure, require or authorise the taking of measures to prevent, manage and respond to cybersecurity threats and incidents, to regulate certain persons in relation to the cybersecurity, and for other related purposes.

Enacted by the Parliament of Naoero as follows:

## PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Naoero Cybersecurity Act 2026.

#### 2 Commencement

This Act commences on the date it is certified by the Speaker.

#### 3 Objectives of this Act

The objectives of this Act are to:

(a) establish a coherent national cybersecurity governance framework through the establishment of the Naoero Cybersecurity Authority, Cybersecurity Advisory Council and CERT;

(b) promote the security and resilience of Naoero’s digital environment through the implementation of the National Cybersecurity Strategy and Roadmap;

(c) safeguard national security, public safety and digital public infrastructure through implementing protective and preventative measures for digital systems;

(d) strengthen national resilience and incident-response capability;

(e) promote trusted digital services and digital economic participation;

(f) protect users, public bodies and businesses from online scams, cyber-enabled fraud and other deceptive digital practices; and

(g) ensure consistency with applicable international cybersecurity standards and obligations.

#### 4 Interpretation

In this Act:

‘access’ means to instruct, communication with, store data in, retrieve data from or otherwise use of any computer system or network;

‘authorised person’ means a person authorised under this Act by the Minister or the Authority to perform functions under this Act;

‘Authority’ means the Naoero Cybersecurity Authority established under Section 5 of this Act;

‘CERT’ means the Naoero Computer Emergency Response Team established under Section 36;

‘critical information infrastructure’ or ‘CII’ means any computer system, network, or information infrastructure, whether physical or virtual, the disruption, compromise or destruction on which would have significant impact on national security, public safety, the economic, public health or essential services in Naoero;

‘computer data’ means any representation of facts, information or concepts in a form suitable for processing in a computer system, including a program capable of causing a computer system to perform a function;

‘Council’ means the Cybersecurity Advisory Council established under Section 12 of this Act;

‘cyber-enabled fraud’ means a dishonest, deceptive or fraudulent act carried out wholly or partly by digital means including through a computer system, digital system, electronic communication, online platform, cloud service, digital identity system or other digital technology, and includes phishing, impersonation, business email compromise, fraudulent investment schemes, romance scams, credential theft, payment redirection scams, malicious links, social engineering and any other deceptive digital practice intended to obtain money, property, personal data, credentials, access, services or any other benefit;

‘cybersecurity’ means the protection and resilience of computer systems, digital systems, networks, information systems, electronic communications, data, digital services, online platforms and digital identity systems, including through measures to preserve confidentiality, integrity, availability and authenticity, and to prevent, detect, monitor, report, respond to, mitigate, recover from, and reduce cyber threats, cybersecurity incidents, unauthorised access, disruption, misuse, interference, modification, disclosure, loss or destruction;

‘cybersecurity incident’ means any event or action with the potential to cause harm or exploit vulnerabilities in a computer system or network and includes online scams, phishing, impersonation, credential theft, fraudulent digital communications, deceptive digital practices or cyber-enabled fraud which involves or affects a computer system, digital system, network, data, digital identity system, electronic communication or online platform;

‘cyber threat’ means any circumstance, event, or action with the potential to cause harm or exploit vulnerabilities in a computer system or network;

‘data’ means computer data and includes traffic data and metadata and includes data stored, processed or transmitted electronically or in a digital form;

‘Department’ means the Department responsible for this Act;

‘digital system’ means combination of hardware, software, networks, data and digital services that process, store, transmit, or manage information using information and communications technologies whether located within or outside Naoero, where such system has an effect within Naoero;

‘essential services’ means services that are necessary for maintaining public welfare safety, economic stability or national security, including but not limited to telecommunications, energy, water, finance, health, and government services;

‘Framework’ means the National Cybersecurity Incident Response Framework established under Section 39;

‘incident response’ means the procedures and actions taken to detect, contain, mitigate, investigate and recover from a cybersecurity incident; s 4LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

‘information system’ means a system for generating, sending, receiving, storing, or otherwise processing data, and includes computer systems and networks;

‘Internet of Things’ or IoT means to the growing network of physical devices that are connected to the internet which can collect, send, or receive data without requiring direct human interaction;

‘Minister’ means the Minister responsible for this Act;

‘National Cybersecurity Strategy’ means the Strategy approved under Section 16 of this Act;

‘operator of CII’ means any person or entity responsible for the management, operation or security of critical information infrastructure;

‘personal data’ means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person;

‘processing’ means any operation or set of operations performed on data, whether or not by automated means, including collection, recording, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, disclosure or destructions;

‘public body’ means a Department, public enterprise, instrumentality, agency or entity, owned or controlled by the Government;

‘Roadmap’ means the National Cybersecurity Roadmap approved under Section 17;

‘Secretary’ means the Secretary for the Department;

‘security measure’ means any administrative, technical, or physical safeguard designed to protect computer systems, networks, or data;

‘traffic data’ means any data relating to a communication by means of a computer system that indicates the origin, destination, route, time, date, size, duration, or type or underlying service; and

‘vulnerability’ means a weakness in the design, implementation, operation, or internal control of a computer system that could be exploited by a cyber threat. s 4 NAOERO CYBERSECURITY ACT 2026

## PART 2 — ADMINISTRATION

### DIVISION 1 — NAOERO CYBERSECURITY AUTHORITY

#### 5 Establishment of Naoero Cybersecurity Authority

(1) The Naoero Cyber Security Authority is established.

(2) The functions and powers of the Authority shall be performed or exercised by the Chief Cybersecurity Officer respectively.

#### 6 Chief Cybersecurity Officer

(1) There shall be established the office of Chief Cybersecurity Officer of the Authority.

(2) A person appointed as the Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall have experience and qualifications in administration and management, cybersecurity or related fields.

(3) The Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall be appointed on terms and conditions by the Cabinet on the recommendation of the Minister.

(4) The Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall be responsible and accountable to the Secretary and Minister for the management of the business and affairs of the Authority.

(5) Where the Chief Cybersecurity Officer is unable to perform the functions of the office for any reason including any official travel, recreation leave or medical leave, the Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall perform the functions of the Chief Cybersecurity Officer for such period as may be necessary.

#### 7 Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer

(1) There shall be established the office of the Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer of the Authority.

(2) A person appointed as the Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall have experience and qualifications in management, cybersecurity or related fields.

(3) The Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall be appointed on terms and conditions by the Cabinet on the recommendation of the Minister.

(4) The Deputy Chief Cybersecurity Officer shall be responsible and accountable to the Chief Cybersecurity Officer for the performance of functions as may be delegated by the Chief Cybersecurity Officer.

#### 8 Staff of the Authority

The staff of the Authority shall be appointed under the Public Service Act 2016.

#### 9 Functions of the Authority

The functions of the Authority are to:

(a) with regard to cybersecurity policy and strategy development:

(i) advance, review and update the national cybersecurity policy, strategy and action plans in line with national security and digital transformation priorities;

(ii) monitor the implementation of the Roadmap and issue;

(iii) advise the Minister and Government on cybersecurity risks, emerging threats and regulatory requirements;

(iv) develop standards, guidelines and codes of practice for the protection of computer systems, networks and data;

(v) promote the adoption of international best practices and standards, adapted to the context and capacity of Naoero;

(vi) facilitate public awareness and capacity-building initiatives to strengthen national cybersecurity resilience; and

(vii) promote prevention, detection, reporting and mitigation of cyber-enabled fraud, including phishing, impersonation, business email compromise, fraudulent investment schemes, romance scams, credential theft, payment-redirection scams, malicious links, social engineering and other deceptive digital practices intended to obtain money, property, personal data, credentials, access, services or any other benefit;

(b) national cybersecurity coordination:

(i) act as the central coordinating body for cybersecurity across public bodies, critical information infrastructure operators and relevant private sector entities;

(ii) establish and maintain mechanisms for information-sharing and cooperation on cyber threats and vulnerabilities;

(iii) coordinate national preparedness, including cybersecurity exercises, risk assessments and contingency planning;

(iv) serve as the national point of contact for regional and international cybersecurity cooperation, including incident coordination and information exchange; and

(v) promote a whole-of-government and multi-stakeholder approach to cybersecurity;

(c) incident response oversight:

(i) establish, oversee or designate a national Computer Emergency Response Team;

(ii) develop and enforce procedures for the reporting, management and resolution of cybersecurity incidents;

(iii) receive and assess incident reports and ensure timely response, containment, mitigation and recovery;

(iv) provide guidance and support to public bodies and critical infrastructure operators during cybersecurity incidents; and

(v) maintain a national repository of cybersecurity incidents for analysis, trend monitoring and policy improvement;

(d) compliance monitoring and enforcement:

(i) monitor compliance with this Act, regulations and applicable cybersecurity standards;

(ii) conduct audits, inspections and assessments of public bodies, service providers and operators of critical information infrastructure;

(iii) issue written directions, advisories or remedial notices to address identified vulnerabilities or non-compliance; s 9LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

(iv) recommend or impose administrative sanctions or penalties in accordance with this Act;

(v) require any person or entity to produce information, records, or documentation relevant to cybersecurity risk or incident;

(vi) enter any premises or access systems of a public body or operator of a CII to conduct inspections, technical assessments or cybersecurity audits;

(vii) require immediate notification and ongoing reporting on cybersecurity incidents, including the provision of technical details necessary for assessment;

(viii) issue urgent directives necessary to protect national security, essential services or public safety including temporary measures affecting the operation of systems or networks;

(ix) accredit, license or authorise cybersecurity service providers, auditors, or professionals where required under this Act or regulations;

(x) engage experts, consultants or external service providers, whether within or outside Naoero, to support the performance of its functions;

(xi) enter into agreements or other arrangements with domestic or international entities for the purpose of exercising its powers under this Act;

(xii) apply to a court for warrants, orders or other legal processes necessary to enforce compliance or exercise powers under this Act;

(xiii) secure, preserve or require the preservation of data or systems relevant to a cybersecurity investigation, subject to any applicable law governing search and seizure; and

(xiv) report periodically to the Minister on national cybersecurity compliance and risk posture; and

(e) implement any other function as may be directed by the Secretary, Minister or the Cabinet respectively.

#### 10 Powers of the Authority

The Authority has the power to do all things necessary or convenient to be done for the performance of its functions under this Act or any other written law.

#### 11 Delegation of functions or powers of the Authority

The Authority may delegate its functions or powers under this Act to a member of the staff or an authorised person.

## PART 3 — NATIONAL CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORK

#### 16 National Cybersecurity Strategy

(1) The Authority shall develop the National Cybersecurity Strategy.

(2) Subject to the approval of the Cabinet, the Strategy shall be made publicly accessible, except for any part that may compromise national security.

(3) The Authority shall be responsible for coordinating the implementation of the Strategy.

(4) The Strategy shall be reviewed annually from the date of its approval by the Cabinet.

(5) Where the Strategy is revised, it shall be approved by the Cabinet before it is published in accordance with this Section.

#### 17 National Cybersecurity Roadmap

(1) The Authority shall develop the National Cybersecurity Roadmap.

(2) The roadmap shall be approved by the Minister, upon the recommendation of the Authority.

(3) A public body shall:

(a) implement activities assigned to it under the Roadmap; and

(b) cooperate with the Authority in its implementation.

(4) The Authority may issue guidance and technical standards to supplement the implementation of the Roadmap.

(5) The Authority shall submit biannual reports to the Minister on the implementation of the Roadmap and review and update the Roadmap once every 3 years.

(6) The Minister may provide policy directions to the Authority regarding the Roadmap.

## PART 4 — CRITICAL INFORMATION INFRASTRUCTURE OR CII

#### 18 Designation of CII

(1) The Authority may designate any system, network, asset, or service as CII for the purposes of this Act in accordance with the criteria under Section 19.

(2) A designation may apply to an affected infrastructure of a:

(a) specific entity;

(b) class of systems or services; or

(c) sector or sub-sector.

#### 19 Criteria for designation

In determining whether to designate a CII, the Authority shall have regard to the:

(a) degree to which the system or service is essential to the functioning of Government, the economy, public safety or national security;

(b) potential impact of disruption, compromise or destruction of the system or service;

(c) extent of interdependency with other systems or sectors;

(d) availability of alternative systems or redundancy measures;

(e) sensitivity and volume of data processed or stored; and

(f) any other matter the Authority considers relevant.

#### 20 Consultation Prior to Designation

(1) Prior to making a designation, the Authority shall, where practicable:

(a) consult the relevant person; and

(b) invite submissions from the affected person.

(2) Where the Authority considers that urgency or national security considerations so require, it may limit its consultation under this Section.

#### 21 Notice of designation

(1) The Authority shall issue a written notice of designation to the relevant person or public body.

(2) The notice shall specify the:

(a) system, service or asset designated;

(b) reasons for the designation;

(c) obligations of the operator of CII arising under this Act; and

(d) effective date of the designation.

(3) The Authority shall maintain a confidential register of designated CII.

#### 22 Review and revocation

(1) The Authority shall annually review designations to ensure their continued relevance.

(2) The Authority may amend or revoke a designation where the:

(a) criteria for designation is no longer met; or

(b) circumstances have materially changed.

#### 23 Rights to request a review of a designation

(1) A person or public body subject to designation may request the Authority to review the designation.

(2) The Authority shall consider the request and notify the person or public body of its decision within 28 days.

#### 24 Operators of CII

Once a designation has been made, the person or public body responsible for the CII shall be deemed an operator of CII and shall comply with the applicable obligations under this Act.

#### 25 Obligations of an operator of CII

An operator of CII shall:

(a) implement and maintain appropriate and proportionate technical and organisational measures to manage cybersecurity risks in accordance with the notice of designation to ensure:

(i) the protection of the confidentiality, integrity and availability of systems and data;

(ii) resilience and continuity of essential services; and

(iii) timely detection of cybersecurity incidents;

(b) establish and maintain a documented cybersecurity risk management programme which shall include:

(i) risk identification and assessment processes;

(ii) risk treatment and mitigation measures;

(iii) regular review and updating procedures; and

(iv) governance and accountability arrangements;

(c) assess risks from third-party ICT providers; and

(d) maintain contingency and exit plans.

#### 26 Security standards and guidelines

The Authority may issue cybersecurity standards, codes of practice, or guidelines applicable to operators of CII who shall comply or demonstrate equivalent measures.

#### 27 Mandatory Incident Reporting

(1) An operator of CII shall notify the Authority of any cybersecurity incident that:

(a) affects, or is likely to affect, the operation of CII; or

(b) compromises sensitive or critical data.

(2) A notification shall be made:

(a) within 72 hours of a general incident occurring; or

(b) immediately where a severe or critical incident has occurred.

#### 28 Incident response

(1) An operator of CII shall:

(a) conduct periodic cybersecurity audits; or

(b) ensure a periodic audit is conducted by an independent auditor. s 23LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

(2) An audit shall assess compliance with this Act.

#### 29 Operator of CII to provide information

(1) The Authority may require an operator of CII to provide information reasonably necessary to monitor compliance.

(2) The operator of CII shall comply and provide the information within the time specified, unless the reason for failing to do so is accepted by the Authority.

(3) Information obtained under this Section shall be treated as confidential and used only for purposes consistent with this Act, except where disclosure is required by law or necessary for cybersecurity response.

#### 30 Inspections

The Authority may conduct an inspection of a system or facility in:

(a) accordance with this Act; and

(b) a manner that minimises disruption to legitimate operations.

#### 31 Authority may issue compliance notice

Where the Authority is satisfied that an operator of CII is not complying with this Act, the Authority may issue a compliance notice requiring the operator of CII to:

(a) take specified remedial action;

(b) implement specific security measures; or

(c) cease non-compliant practice.

non-compliance under this Part

## PART 5 — PUBLIC DIGITAL SYSTEMS SECURITY

#### 33 Protection of public digital systems

A public body shall:

(a) ensure that a digital system operated by or on behalf of the Government:

(i) complies with national cybersecurity standards, policies, and guidelines issued by the Authority; and

(ii) incorporates cybersecurity measures at the design, development, procurement, and implementation stages, including the application of secure-by-design and secure-by-default; and

(b) take reasonable steps to ensure that cybersecurity requirements are integrated into:

(i) system specifications and procurement processes;

(ii) contractual arrangements with service providers; and

(iii) system life cycle management, including maintenance and upgrades.

#### 34 Security of digital systems storing identity data

A public body shall:

(a) ensure that a digital system storing identity data operated by or on behalf of Government, incorporates appropriate and proportionate security measures, including but not limited to:

(i) the use of encryption to protect identity data in storage and transmission;

(ii) safeguards to ensure accurate and reliable identity verification, that include measures to prevent unauthorised access, identity fraud, and impersonation; and

(iii) privacy protections to ensure that personal data is collected, used, stored and disclosed in a lawful, secure and proportionate manner.

(b) implement technical and organisational measures to:

(i) maintain the integrity and confidentiality of identity data;

(ii) restrict access to authorised persons only; and

(iii) ensure traceability and accountability in the use of such digital system.

#### 35 Cloud and data storage governance

(1) A public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity that stores, processes, transmits, or manages data using cloud services shall implement appropriate cybersecurity measures to protect the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of such data and systems.

(2) Prior to adopting or substantially changing a cloud service, a public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity shall undertake a cybersecurity risk assessment having regard to:

(a) the sensitivity and classification of the data;

(b) risks relating to unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, loss, or disruption;

(c) business continuity and disaster recovery requirements; and

(d) any national security considerations determined by the Authority.

(3) The Authority may require a public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity to provide information reasonably necessary to assess compliance with this Section.

(4) Regulations may prescribe categories of Government data or critical information infrastructure data that shall comply with additional cybersecurity, storage, hosting or data-sovereignty requirements. s 35LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 6 — CYBERSECURITY INCIDENT RESPONSE

#### 36 Establishment of the Naoero Computer Emergency Response

Team

(1) The Naoero Computer Emergency Response Team or CERT is established.

(2) The CERT shall be accountable and report to the Authority for the performance of its functions and exercise of its powers.

(3) The CERT shall comprise of staff members of the Authority, as may be designated by the Authority.

(4) The CERT shall be the national operational body responsible for cybersecurity incident response, coordination, technical assistance, and cyber threat information sharing.

#### 37 Functions of the CERT

The functions of the CERT include:

(a) receiving, monitoring, analysing, and responding to reports of cybersecurity incidents;

(b) coordinating the management of cybersecurity incidents affecting digital systems of the Government, CII and other digital systems;

(c) providing technical advice and assistance to public bodies, CII operators, businesses, and individuals in relation to cybersecurity incidents;

(d) issuing alerts, advisories, warnings, indicators of compromise, public guidance and recommended mitigation measures relating to cybersecurity threats, cybersecurity incidents, online scams, phishing, impersonation, fraudulent digital communication, business email compromise, credential theft and other cyber-enabled deceptive practices;

(e) facilitating the sharing of cyber threat intelligence and cybersecurity information;

(f) coordinating national responses to significant cybersecurity incidents;

(g) supporting cybersecurity preparedness, training, exercises, and awareness activities;

(h) maintaining incident reporting and response capabilities on a continuous basis;

(i) cooperate and exchange information for the purpose of implementing cybersecurity measures, with:

(i) law enforcement agencies;

(ii) public bodies;

(iii) operators or CII;

(iv) foreign counterpart computer emergency response teams;

(v) regional and international cybersecurity organisations; and

(vi) any other person or entity necessary for the effective management of cybersecurity risks and incidents; and

(j) any other function as may be directed by the Authority or prescribed.

#### 38 Powers of the CERT

The CERT has the power to do all things necessary or convenient to be done for the performance of its functions under this Act or any other written law.

#### 39 Naoero Incident Response Framework

(1) The Authority shall establish and maintain a National Cybersecurity Incident Response Framework.

(2) The Framework shall provide for the coordinated prevention, detection, reporting, assessment, containment, mitigation, response, recovery and post-incident review of cybersecurity incidents including:

(a) incident classification levels and severity ratings;

(b) incident reporting procedures and timelines;

(c) roles and responsibilities of public bodies, operators of CII and other relevant entities;

(d) coordination arrangements between the CERT and such agencies, entities or bodies under Section 37(i);

(e) information sharing mechanisms during cybersecurity incidents;

(f) procedures for responding to significant cybersecurity incidents and cyber emergencies;

(g) business continuity and recovery measures;

(h) preservation of evidence and forensic requirements; and

(i) post-incident analysis for improved response.

(3) Public bodies, operators of CII and entities as may be prescribed shall comply with the Framework.

(4) Where the Authority determines that a significant cybersecurity incident or cyber emergency exists, the CERT may, subject to the direction and oversight of the Authority, coordinate incident response activities and issue such operational and technical directions as are reasonably necessary to contain, mitigate, analyse, or respond to the incident or emergency.

(5) For the purposes of subsection (4), operational and technical directions may include directions relating to information sharing, coordination of response activities, implementation of containment or mitigation measures, technical investigations, threat monitoring, and other actions necessary to support an effective incident response.

(6) For the avoidance of doubt, the directions issued by CERT under subsection (4) shall exclude the exercise of regulatory, compliance, enforcement, sanctioning, or other coercive powers unless expressly authorised by this Act or pursuant to a written delegation by the Authority.

(7) Any regulatory, compliance, or enforcement direction issued under this Section shall be made by the Authority, which shall retain overall responsibility for the management and oversight of significant cybersecurity incidents and cyber emergencies.

(8) The CERT shall exercise its functions under this Section in accordance with any directions, policies, or procedures issued by the Authority and shall promptly report to the Authority on measures taken under this Section.

(9) A person or entity subject to an operational or technical direction shall provide such assistance, information or cooperation as may reasonably be required by the CERT for the purposes of responding to a cybersecurity incident.

(10) The Authority may issue standards, guidelines and procedures for the implementation of the Framework. s 39LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 7 — COMPLIANCE, AUDITS AND CODES OF PRACTICE

#### 40 Tiered Compliance Model

(1) The Authority may establish and administer a risk-based tiered compliance model for the purposes of this Act.

(2) The tiered compliance model may classify entities according to their cybersecurity risk profile, size, functions, or significance to national security, public services, economic stability, or CII.

(3) Without limiting subsection (2), the Authority may prescribe reporting requirements, audit requirements and compliance measures for different classes of entities.

(4) In determining the applicable tier for an entity, the Authority shall have regard to:

(a) the nature and sensitivity of the information processed;

(b) the potential impact of a cybersecurity incident;

(c) whether the entity owns, operates or supports CII;

(d) the scale and complexity of the entity’s digital systems; and

(e) any other prescribed matter.

(5) The Authority shall ensure that compliance obligations imposed under this Act are proportionate to the cybersecurity risks presented by the relevant entity.

(6) The Authority may review and reclassify an entity where circumstances materially change.

#### 41 Cybersecurity Audits

(1) The Authority may require a public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity to undertake a cybersecurity audit to assess:

(a) compliance with this Act;

(b) compliance with applicable cybersecurity standards, regulations, directions or codes of practice;

(c) cybersecurity governance arrangements;

(d) technical, organisational, and operational security measures;

(e) incident response capabilities; and

(f) any other matter relevant to cybersecurity risk management.

(2) A cybersecurity audit shall be conducted:

(a) by an independent auditor approved by the Authority; or

(b) in such other manner as may be prescribed by regulations.

(3) The Authority may require an audited entity to provide information, records, documents, access to systems or other assistance reasonably necessary for the conduct of an audit.

(4) Upon completion of an audit, the Authority may issue recommendations, directives or corrective action requirements.

(5) A public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity shall take reasonable steps to address any material cybersecurity deficiencies identified through the audit process.

(6) The Authority may establish audit frequencies and requirements based on the tiered compliance model established under Section 40.

#### 42 Codes of Practice

(1) The Authority may issue, approve, adopt, amend or revoke cybersecurity codes of practice for the purposes of this Act.

(2) A code of practice may provide practical guidance relating to:

(a) cybersecurity governance and risk management;

(b) protection of government systems;

(c) protection of CII;

(d) cloud and data governance;

(e) cybersecurity incident reporting and response;

(f) supply chain and third-party risk management;

(g) cybersecurity awareness and training;

(h) vulnerability management; and

(i) any other cybersecurity matter considered necessary by the NCA.

(3) Compliance with an applicable code of practice may be taken into account by the Authority in determining whether a person or entity has complied with this Act.

(4) The Authority may require a public body, operator of CII or prescribed entity to comply with a code of practice issued under this Section.

(5) The Authority shall publish an approved code of practice in such a manner as it considers appropriate.

(6) A code of practice issued under this Section shall be admissible in any proceedings under this Act as evidence of adequate cybersecurity practice. s 42LAWS OF THE REPUBLIC OF NAOERO

## PART 8 — DATA PROTECTION

#### 43 Protection of Personal Data

(1) A public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity that collects, stores, processes or transmits personal data shall implement reasonable technical and organisational measures to protect such data against unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, loss, destruction or misuse.

(2) The measures under subsection (1) shall be proportionate to the nature, sensitivity and volume of the personal data concerned.

(3) The Authority may issue standards, guidelines or directions relating to the protection of personal data for cybersecurity purposes.

#### 44 Data Breach Notification

(1) A public body, operator of CII, or other prescribed entity that becomes aware of a data breach involving personal data shall, without undue delay, notify the Authority where such breach is likely to result in harm to an individual, public safety, national security or the security of a digital system.

(2) A notification under subsection (1) shall include, to the extent known, the:

(a) nature of the breach;

(b) categories of personal data affected;

(c) likely consequences of the breach; and

(d) measures taken or proposed to address the breach.

(3) Where the data breach is likely to result in a significant risk of harm to affected individuals, the entity shall take reasonable steps to notify those individuals as soon as practicable.

(4) The Authority may issue guidelines relating to data breach notification procedures and timeframes.

(5) Compliance with this Section does not relieve a person or entity of any obligation imposed under any other written law.

## PART 9 — APPLICATION OF ACT WITH THE CYBERCRIME ACT 2015

#### 45 Act does not limit the Cybercrime Act 2015

(1) This Act shall not limit, affect or replace the operation of the Cybercrime Act 2015 or any other written law relating to criminal investigations, offences or prosecution of cybercrime.

(2) Where the Authority or the CERT becomes aware that a cybersecurity incident may constitute an offence under the Cybercrime Act 2015 or any other written law, the matter shall be referred to the Naoero Police Force.

(3) The Authority, CERT and law enforcement agencies may cooperate and exchange information for the purposes of preventing, detecting, investigating and responding to a cybercrime, subject to any applicable law.

#### 46 Digital Evidence

(1) A person or entity that becomes aware of a cybersecurity incident shall take reasonable steps to preserve relevant digital evidence relating to such incident.

(2) The Authority or CERT may issue directions or guidelines regarding the preservation, handling, collection, storage and transfer of digital evidence for cybersecurity purposes.

(3) Where a cybersecurity incident is suspected to involve a criminal offence, any relevant digital evidence shall be preserved and made available to law enforcement agencies.

(4) Nothing in this Section authorises the Authority or CERT to exercise powers of criminal investigation except as expressly provided by law.

## PART 10 — CAPACITY BUILDING

#### 47 Cybersecurity Awareness Programmes

(1) The Authority shall promote and support cybersecurity awareness programmes to enhance public understanding of cybersecurity risks, safe online practices and national cyber resilience.

(2) An awareness programme promoted under this Section shall, where practicable, provide practical guidance for the identification, avoidance and reporting of online scams, phishing, impersonation, fraudulent investment schemes, romance scams, business email compromise, malicious links, credential theft, payment-redirection scams and other social-engineering activities.

(3) For the purposes of subsection (1), the Authority may:

(a) conduct public awareness campaigns;

(b) develop educational materials and guidance;

(c) support cybersecurity awareness initiatives in schools, workplaces and communities; and

(d) collaborate with public bodies, private sector entities, civil society organisations and regional or international partners.

#### 48 Cybersecurity Workforce Development

(1) The Authority shall promote the development of a skilled cybersecurity workforce.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), the Authority may:

(a) support cybersecurity education, training and professional development programmes;

(b) facilitate cybersecurity exercises, workshops and certification opportunities;

(c) collaborate with educational institutions, industry and regional or international partners; and

(d) encourage the development and retention of cybersecurity expertise within the public and private sectors.

(3) The Authority may develop and implement a National Cybersecurity Capacity-Building Strategy.

## PART 11 — INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION

#### 49 Cross-Border Cooperation

(1) The Authority and the CERT may cooperate with foreign counterparts, foreign computer emergency response teams, regional and international organisations and other competent authorities for the purposes of cybersecurity, cyber resilience, cyber threat intelligence sharing, incident response, capacity building and the protection of CII.

(2) Cooperation under subsection (1) may include:

(a) the exchange of cybersecurity information and technical expertise;

(b) responding to cross-border cybersecurity incidents;

(c) participation in regional and international cybersecurity initiatives, exercises and networks; and

(d) any other activity consistent with the purposes of this Act.

(3) Any cooperation under this Section shall be undertaken in accordance with the laws of Naoero and any applicable international agreement or arrangement.

(4) Nothing in this Section authorises the disclosure of information where such disclosure would prejudice national security, public safety, an ongoing investigation or any other protected interest under the laws of Naoero.

## PART 12 — ENFORCEMENT

#### 50 Authority may conduct inspection

(1) For the purpose of monitoring compliance with this Act, the Authority may require a public body, operator of CII, or other prescribed entity to provide information, records, documents or other material relevant to compliance with this Act.

(2) An authorised person of the Authority may, upon reasonable notice and during normal business hours, enter premises occupied by a public body, operator of CII, or other prescribed entity to conduct an inspection relating to compliance with this Act.

(3) A person shall not obstruct, hinder or refuse to cooperate with an authorised person acting in the lawful exercise of powers under this Section.

(4) Nothing in this Section authorises the Authority to exercise investigative powers.

#### 51 Administrative Penalties for non-compliance with this Act

(1) Where the Authority is satisfied that a public body, operator of CII or other prescribed entity has failed to comply with this Act, the Authority may impose an administrative penalty provided that the Authority provides the affected entity with a:

(a) notice of the alleged non-compliance; and

(b) reasonable opportunity to show cause as to why such penalty should not be imposed.

(2) In determining whether to impose an administrative penalty and the amount of the penalty, the Authority shall consider:

(a) the nature and seriousness of the non-compliance;

(b) the actual or potential impact of the non-compliance;

(c) whether the non-compliance was repeated or persistent; and

(d) any steps taken to remedy the non-compliance.

(3) The Authority may impose the following administrative penalties:

(a) a prescribed monetary fine not exceeding $10,000;

(b) a prescribed fee for non-compliance with the Authority’s direction not exceeding $3,000;

(c) a prescribed cost recovery amount for audits or other actions undertaken by the Authority not exceeding 10,000; or

(d) a prescribed mandatory corrective measure.

(4) The Authority may, in lieu of or in addition to an administrative penalty, issue a warning or compliance notice.

#### 52 Offences

(1) A person shall not:

(a) intentionally provide false or misleading information to the Authority; or

(b) wilfully obstruct the Authority in the performance of its functions and exercise of its powers.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction:

(a) for an individual to a fine not exceeding $20,000 or an imprisonment term not exceeding 1 year or both; and

(b) for a corporation, to a fine not exceeding $100,000.

## PART 13 — MISCELLANEOUS

#### 54 Emerging Technologies

(1) The Authority shall monitor and assess cybersecurity risks associated with emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence, cloud computing, digital identity systems, the Internet of Things and other technologies relevant to the cybersecurity of Naoero.

(2) The Authority may issue guidelines, advisories, standards, or recommendations relating to the secure use and management of emerging technologies.

(3) In performing its function under this Section, the Authority may consult public bodies, industry stakeholders, technical experts and regional or international organisations.

#### 55 Annual Report

The Minister may submit an annual Report on the implementation of this Act including the outcomes of the implementation of the Strategy and the Roadmap.

#### 56 Protection from Liability

The Authority or any other person authorised under this Act, shall not be liable for any act done or omitted to be done in good faith in the performance of a function or exercise of a power under this Act or any other written law in furthering the objective of the Act.

#### 57 Regulations

(1) The Cabinet may make regulations for all matters required or necessary to give effect to this Act.

(2) Without limiting subsection (1), regulations may be made to provide for:

(a) what constitutes as a general, severe or critical cybersecurity incidents;

(b) procedures necessary for the Authority to perform its functions and exercise its powers;

(c) procedures for the prevention, reporting, coordination, public warning, mitigation and referral of cyber-enabled fraud, including phishing, impersonation, business email compromise, fraudulent investment schemes, romance scams, credential theft, payment redirection scams, malicious links, social engineering and any other deceptive digital practice intended to obtain money, property, personal data, credentials, access, services or any other benefit;

(d) administrative penalties; and

(e) fees for the purposes of the Act.
